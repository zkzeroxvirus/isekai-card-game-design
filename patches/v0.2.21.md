# v0.2.21 — Society, Economy & Reputation Refactor

## Summary

This patch expands the project's living-world architecture so the player can participate in fantasy society as more than an adventurer.

The central addition is a cohesive civilian-world model covering:

- economy
- shops and trade
- professions and ordinary work
- reputation
- NPC memory
- information propagation
- crime and law
- citizenship and residency
- property
- construction
- businesses
- settlement development

These do **not** become a new seventh progression domain. They live primarily inside the existing **World** domain and reuse Skills, Jobs, relationships, money, hidden progression, and world state.

## New Canon Rules

- **Every profession participates in the same world rather than existing in its own minigame bubble.**
- **Skills determine capability. Jobs recognize role. Reputation records track record. The World provides opportunity.**
- **The world remembers what happened, but people only react to what they know or believe.**
- **Reputation is not morality.**
- **Simulate causes deeply enough that consequences feel earned; abstract everything the player does not need to meaningfully understand or influence.**

## Economy

Settlements may track compact economic categories such as:

- Food
- Raw Materials
- Crafted Goods
- Medicine
- Labor
- Housing
- Trade Access
- Security
- Prosperity

Economic state can influence prices, stock, work opportunities, construction, faction projects, migration, shortages, and narrative events.

The economy is designed to generate stories and understandable consequences rather than exhaustive commodity simulation.

## Stores

Stores now conceptually depend on:

- stock
- specialization
- supply relationships
- local demand
- merchant wealth
- proprietor relationship
- legality
- faction control
- production knowledge
- world state

Merchants do not require infinite purchasing power or perfectly static inventories.

## Work and Professions

Ordinary work is explicitly supported as a way to enter society.

Examples include caravan labor, workshop assistance, medicine, cooking, gathering, construction, messenger work, surveying, hunting, inventory work, and illicit logistics.

Work can produce money, Skill progress, Job qualification, relationships, rumors, reputation, hidden progression, and adventure hooks.

Profession-oriented Jobs should eventually support recognizable work from the profession.

## Reliability and Performance

Accepted work can create social commitments.

The game can remember:

- whether the player appeared
- whether they completed the task
- whether they warned/cancelled appropriately
- speed
- care
- quality
- collateral damage
- use or theft of supplied materials
- whether they overdelivered

Repeated patterns can produce reputational traits such as Reliable, Efficient, Thorough, Careless, Professional, Discreet, or Unreliable.

## Reputation

Reputation now has three primary scales:

1. **Personal Reputation** — what individual NPCs think and remember.
2. **Group Reputation** — how factions, settlements, guilds, professions, churches, clans, and other communities regard the player.
3. **Public Reputation** — what broader society has heard or believes.

These can disagree.

Reputation derives from evidence and context rather than one universal score.

## Information Propagation

The game distinguishes:

**What happened**

from

**What people know or believe happened.**

Information may spread through witnesses, guild records, guards, merchants, family, rumors, factions, clergy, criminal networks, magical communication, and other setting-appropriate channels.

This prevents omniscient reputation updates and supports secrets, misinformation, false accusation, hidden villainy, and public mythmaking.

## Crime and Law

Crime now depends on:

- local law
- witnesses
- evidence
- victim status
- faction control
- jurisdiction
- player legal status
- investigation capability

Possible consequences include fines, restitution, arrest, confiscation, exile, license loss, citizenship loss, bounties, trials, faction retaliation, and blackmail.

## Citizenship and Residency

The player can progress socially from outsider toward belonging.

Possible statuses include:

- Unregistered Foreigner
- Registered Traveler
- Temporary Resident
- Permanent Resident
- Citizen / Subject
- Licensed Professional
- Landholder
- Business Owner

Legal status can affect property, guild access, taxes, training, political participation, licenses, weapon rights, institutional Jobs, and legal protection.

## Property and Construction

Property can include rooms, homes, workshops, shops, forges, warehouses, farms, frontier claims, and estates.

Construction uses plots, renovations, civic projects, district projects, or frontier claims rather than unrestricted survival-game free-building by default.

Projects may require permission, land, plans, materials, labor, specialists, money, time, and security.

## Businesses

Advanced civilian progression may allow player-owned or operated:

- forges
- taverns
- clinics
- shops
- warehouses
- caravan companies
- farms
- expedition companies

Business ownership should create decisions while supporting delegation so it does not become mandatory spreadsheet management.

## Hearthcross Prototype Scope

The first vertical slice should prove only a thin layer:

1. two distinct shops
2. one ordinary work opportunity
3. one reliability consequence
4. one professional-quality consequence
5. one named NPC memory
6. one propagated reputation event
7. one legal-status step
8. one settlement economic variable affecting stock, price, or work
9. one Job/Skill changing a civilian interaction

Full property, advanced business management, complex taxation, and large-scale economic simulation remain deferred.
