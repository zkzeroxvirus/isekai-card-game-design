# v0.2.11 — Origins, Skills & System Perception

**Date:** 2026-08-13

## Added — Origin-Driven Onboarding

- Origins now affect arrival method, memory state, starting Skills, combat experience, divine involvement, narrative opportunities, and system perception.
- Added **the Threshold** as a structural in-between state that may appear as a god, representative, bureaucratic world function, unknowable presence, symbols/machinery, or a forgotten encounter.
- Added provisional Origin families: Summoned, Reincarnated, Accidental Transfer, Failed Hero Summoning, Divine Bargain, and System Anomaly.

## Added — Skills

- Defined **Skills** as broad persistent capabilities rather than cards.
- Skills may affect combat, exploration, crafting, social interaction, perception, survival, progression, and narrative choices.
- Formalized the hierarchy: **Skill → Weapon Art → Codex Manifestation** where appropriate.
- Skills can be earned through use, training, study, survival, hidden conditions, equipment, factions, observation, divine intervention, or forbidden acquisition.
- Advanced/hidden class qualification can require combinations of Skills and accomplishments.

## Added — System Comprehension

- Added **System Comprehension** as the working term for the character's ability to perceive and interpret supernatural game systems.
- System Comprehension may change HUD precision, numerical visibility, status detail, Skill progress, manifestation metadata, hidden prerequisites, resistances, Authority signatures, and other normally inaccessible information.
- UI information itself can now function as progression.

## Added — Perception Lenses

- The Transference may translate the same world systems differently depending on prior experience.
- MMO-literate, tabletop-literate, and non-gamer characters can perceive equivalent mechanics through different conceptual language.
- Different perception lenses must remain compatible with one authoritative multiplayer simulation.

## Changed — Divine Codex Reveal

- The player does not need to begin with full knowledge of the Active Codex.
- Early play should emphasize Foundation Actions, Weapon Arts, starting Skills, physical vulnerability, and a deliberately limited HUD.
- The first manifestation should be a narrative discovery.
- Origin can affect when and how the Codex is recognized.

## Changed — Starting Power

- Default otherworlders begin vulnerable.
- Relevant real-world combat experience or a combat-focused starting Skill can improve the starting baseline.
- Strong starting advantages should carry opportunity costs, obligations, narrative burdens, or weaker access elsewhere rather than becoming strictly optimal Origins.

## Added — First 30 Minutes

All Origin openings should teach a shared spine:

1. movement and camera
2. interaction
3. physical danger
4. weapon use and basic Weapon Arts
5. first Skill acquisition/recognition
6. one meaningful threat
7. reaching a stable objective
8. realizing the player's understanding of the world is incomplete

Origin-specific openings may present this through radically different scenarios.

## Locked Principles

- Origins change both character history and system perception.
- Skills are broader than cards.
- System information can be learned.
- The HUD should expand with understanding.
- The Divine Codex should be discovered through play.
- Origin differences should matter narratively and mechanically without fragmenting multiplayer rules.
