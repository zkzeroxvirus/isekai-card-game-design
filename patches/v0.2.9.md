# v0.2.9 — Visual Direction

## Summary

Established the project's production-facing visual target as **stylized anime fantasy with grounded environments and increasingly supernatural visual escalation**.

The goal is to create a coherent visual identity for a third-person multiplayer action-RPG/deckbuilder without committing to either full photorealism or full cel shading.

## Rendering Direction

- Selected **softly stylized PBR** as the provisional rendering approach.
- Characters use anime-influenced proportions, faces, silhouettes, hairstyles, and expression.
- Environments and materials remain comparatively grounded to preserve contrast with supernatural progression.
- Full photoreal human rendering is not the target.
- Full cel shading is not currently locked.

## New Visual Principle

**Ground the world before breaking it.**

The intended escalation is:

**Practical fantasy → refined supernatural craft → legendary manifestations → divine intervention → Authority-driven reality distortion.**

## Character Direction

- Slightly idealized heroic anatomy.
- Anime-influenced facial proportions and expressive features.
- Strong silhouettes and readable equipment.
- Clean material and skin treatment rather than extreme realistic microdetail.
- Character customization must preserve animation quality and combat readability.

## Environment Direction

- Early environments emphasize danger, scale, modest settlements, worn construction, practical equipment, and believable fantasy materials.
- Late environments may expose impossible geometry, system glyphs, divine structures, spatial anomalies, and broken world-law visuals.
- Atmospheric lighting is a major component of environmental identity.

## Equipment Progression

Equipment progression should be physically visible through changes to:

- silhouette
- materials
- rune treatment
- monster components
- animation
- geometry
- aura/state effects

Authority-touched equipment should alter reality or material behavior rather than merely adding more glow.

## Combat & VFX

Added a visual hierarchy for:

- Mortal Techniques
- Arts / advanced skills
- Spells
- Evolved abilities
- Blessings / Miracles
- Authorities

Authorities should visually manipulate rules, concepts, space, time, death, contracts, or similar world systems rather than behave like oversized normal spells.

## Multiplayer Readability

Combat readability takes priority over spectacle.

Required principles include:

- enemy telegraphs remain visible through allied VFX
- hostile and allied persistent areas use distinct visual language
- boss mechanics take priority over player spectacle
- summons should not obscure critical targets
- critical reaction/counter windows use consistent treatment
- screen shake, flashes, bloom, and VFX density should eventually be configurable

## Divine Codex UI

- The combat interface represents a supernatural soul-bound system rather than literal physical cards.
- Codex presentation may use translucent panels, geometric sigils, layered depth, animated borders, divine script, and manifestation effects.
- Live combat cards prioritize icon, name, input, cost, targeting state, and critical information rather than large text blocks.
- UI may evolve as player knowledge grows, revealing deeper system metadata.
- Forbidden/corrupted manifestations may distort the interface while remaining usable and readable.

## Faction & Settlement Visual Identity

Major factions require recognizable visual language across architecture, heraldry, equipment, materials, iconography, NPC silhouettes, and magical motifs.

Settlement progression and host-world divergence must be physically visible.

Example directions include prosperous, militarized, occult, or mercantile outcomes, but these are not fixed morality templates.

## Monster Direction

- Monsters prioritize recognizable silhouette and role readability over generic realism.
- Variants should change equipment, posture, animation, supernatural traits, and silhouette rather than rely primarily on recolors.
- Early monsters may remain visually modest while still being mechanically dangerous.

## Camera Direction

- Default perspective is third-person action-RPG.
- Camera remains close enough for character/equipment identity but wide enough for cooperative combat awareness.
- Optional lock-on and targeting adjustments are expected.
- Avoid frequent forced cinematic camera takeover during multiplayer combat.

## Narrative Presentation

Full cinematics are reserved for major story beats, bosses, divine encounters, and era transitions.

Routine narrative content should rely on scalable in-engine conversation, staging, posing, facial animation, and camera composition systems.

## Production Guardrails

Favor reusable visual systems for:

- modular character equipment
- faction kits
- settlement-state changes
- VFX families
- Codex templates
- dungeon kits
- monster-family variants

Stylization should reduce asset-production burden rather than require photoreal assets plus an additional style layer.

## Updated Proof-of-Concept Goal

The technical proof of concept now also validates:

- one stylized player character
- one grounded fantasy environment
- one equipment evolution example
- one enemy visual variant
- Technique, Spell, and Reaction VFX
- one Authority-style reality-distortion effect
- one five-card Divine Codex HUD
- keyboard/controller glyph presentation
- one faction/environment visual motif

Success condition:

**Anime-influenced characters, softly stylized PBR environments, supernatural Codex UI, readable real-time card combat, and multiplayer VFX must feel like parts of the same game.**

## Deliberately Unresolved

- exact shader implementation
- final concept-art style
- outline/rim-light usage
- final palette/color script
- post-processing profile
- Codex typography and glyph language
- faction-specific visual identities
- pantheon visual language
- character creator scope
- final camera/FOV values
- animation exaggeration level
- platform-specific VFX budgets
