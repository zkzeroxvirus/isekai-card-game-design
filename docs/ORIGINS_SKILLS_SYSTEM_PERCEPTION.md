# Origins, Skills & System Perception

**Design Version: v0.2.11**

## Core Principle

**Origins change both who the character is and how the world is interpreted.**

The player's method of arrival, previous-world experience, memory state, divine involvement, and personal history can change starting Skills, early vulnerability, narrative opportunities, initial HUD clarity, progression language, and the timing/form of Divine Codex discovery.

## The Threshold

All otherworlder Origins pass through some form of intermediate state before entering the world. Working term: **the Threshold**.

The Threshold is a shared structural concept, not a single identical scene.

Depending on Origin, the player might encounter:

- a recognizable god
- a divine representative
- a bureaucratic world function
- an artificial or administrative intelligence
- a faceless psychopomp
- an unknowable presence
- symbols or machinery without a speaker
- a false human appearance used by something nonhuman
- nothing the character can consciously remember

The common element is contact with the world's deeper system and the possibility of **Skills**.

## Origins

An Origin can encode:

- previous world
- previous occupation/background
- method of Transference
- whether the character died, was summoned, reincarnated, displaced, or transferred accidentally
- memory retention
- vessel/body circumstances
- divine patronage or interference
- combat experience
- game/system literacy
- magical compatibility
- social baggage or faction ties
- anomalous Codex behavior
- Threshold experience
- starting Skills
- starting System Comprehension

Origins should meaningfully change early play without creating a single objectively best option.

## Example Origin Families

### Summoned

The character arrives through an intentional ritual.

Likely traits:

- receives explanation and social context early
- may begin with equipment or institutional support
- is immediately entangled in politics or expectations
- may be recognized by specific factions

### Reincarnated

The character is reborn into the world or a new vessel.

Likely traits:

- may possess partial native context
- can have mixed old/new memories
- progression may reveal itself gradually
- can blur the distinction between native and otherworlder perception

### Accidental Transfer

The character arrives without a known summoner or patron.

Likely traits:

- survival-heavy opening
- minimal explanation
- greater mystery around the Transference
- potentially unusual narrative reactions

### Failed Hero Summoning

The character was intended for a larger role but the process failed, misidentified them, or produced an unexpected result.

Likely traits:

- strong mystery hook
- possible resentment or political danger
- unusual Codex behavior
- access to information or factions connected to the failed ritual

### Divine Bargain

The character remembers direct negotiation at the Threshold.

Likely traits:

- can choose or receive a powerful starting Skill
- gains clear early supernatural context
- also receives obligations, enemies, restrictions, or divine attention

### System Anomaly

The character's arrival does not conform to known Transference patterns.

Likely traits:

- corrupted, incomplete, or unusually revealing interface
- access to hidden information
- direct ties to endgame mysteries

This Origin family should likely be locked behind later play because it can expose major setting truths.

## Skills

**Skill** is the broadest persistent unit of learned capability.

A Skill is not automatically a card.

Skills can affect combat, exploration, crafting, social interaction, perception, survival, progression, narrative choices, and Codex behavior.

Examples:

- Swordsmanship
- Shield Mastery
- Archery
- Mana Perception
- Appraisal
- Cooking
- Tracking
- Deception
- Negotiation
- Medicine
- Pain Tolerance
- Research
- Cartography
- Monster Lore
- Stealth
- Smithing

## Skill → Weapon Art → Manifestation

The intended capability hierarchy is:

**Skills** — persistent knowledge, proficiency, adaptation, or supernatural capability.

**Weapon Arts** — reliable trained expressions enabled by weapon family, Skills, discipline, and mastery.

**Codex Manifestations** — exceptional expressions that enter the Active Codex as cards and provide tactical or supernatural opportunities.

A character may know Swordsmanship without possessing every sword manifestation. A manifestation may represent a rare, refined, evolved, or metaphysically recognized expression of a broader Skill.

## Skill Acquisition

Skills should primarily be discovered, learned, earned, inherited, or recognized rather than purchased from a generic point tree.

Possible acquisition sources:

- repeated meaningful use
- training with an NPC
- books or research
- surviving unusual circumstances
- hidden-condition fulfillment
- divine gifts
- class trials
- equipment mastery
- observation or imitation
- faction instruction
- achievements
- monster knowledge
- Origin inheritance
- forbidden copying or theft

### Example Recognition

> Condition recognized: continued combat while suffering untreated wounds.
>
> **SKILL ACQUIRED — Pain Tolerance I**

### Design Rule

Skill acquisition should reward meaningful behavior and discovery, not trivial repetition or easily automated grinding.

## Skills and Class Qualification

Advanced disciplines/classes can require combinations of Skills and accomplishments.

Example:

Swordsmanship III + Footwork II + Battle Sense II → Swordmaster qualification

Shield Mastery III + Interception II + Resolve I → Guardian qualification

Mana Perception II + Swordsmanship II + Elemental Channeling II → Spellblade qualification

This supports hidden classes that emerge from a character's actual history rather than a visible universal class tree.

## System Comprehension

Working term: **System Comprehension**.

System Comprehension represents how accurately the character can perceive and interpret the supernatural systems governing progression, Skills, manifestations, enemies, and world laws.

It can affect the HUD itself.

### Low Comprehension

The player may receive:

- vague health feedback
- minimal or no numerical stats
- qualitative enemy danger cues
- hidden Skill progress
- limited status information

### Developing Comprehension

The player may begin to see:

- numerical health/resource values
- named statuses
- Skill ranks
- mastery progress
- card costs/tags
- enemy traits

### High Comprehension

The player may uncover:

- hidden prerequisites
- exact resistance information
- manifestation provenance
- evolution requirements
- Authority signatures
- unusual world-state metadata
- information normally inaccessible to mortals

System Comprehension is both a UI system and a lore/progression system.

## Perception Lens

The Transference can translate the same underlying world mechanics through concepts familiar to the individual.

Examples:

### MMO-Literate Origin

May interpret progression as:

- Level
- Experience
- Skill proficiency
- threat indicators
- cooldown-like timing

### Tabletop-Literate Origin

May interpret progression as:

- attributes
- checks
- proficiencies
- conditions
- disciplines

### Non-Gamer Origin

May initially perceive:

- instinct
- fatigue
- confidence
- vague readiness
- symbolic or sensory cues rather than explicit numbers

The underlying mechanics remain compatible; the presentation language differs.

### Design Rule

Perception differences should create flavor, discovery, and selective information advantages without requiring fundamentally incompatible multiplayer simulations.

## Gradual Divine Codex Reveal

The player should not necessarily begin the game knowing they are playing a deck-driven action RPG.

Early play can expose only:

- movement
- interaction
- limited HUD
- Foundation Actions
- one weapon family
- basic Weapon Arts
- starting Skills

The first manifestation should feel like a narrative discovery.

Example sequence:

1. The player survives using ordinary movement and Weapon Arts.
2. A dangerous situation satisfies a hidden condition.
3. A new symbol or interface fragment appears.
4. The player invokes a manifestation for the first time.
5. The character does not fully understand what happened.
6. Repeated discoveries eventually reveal the Active Codex and its five-slot hand.

Possible first manifestation:

**Desperate Parry**

The exact reveal timing may vary by Origin.

## Origin-Dependent Codex Revelation

Examples:

- A System-Familiar character may identify manifestations early.
- A Divinely Sponsored character may receive explicit explanation.
- A Failed Summon may have fragmented or damaged UI.
- A Reincarnated character may initially experience Skills in a native cultural framework.
- An Anomalous character may see information other players cannot normally access.

Origins can therefore alter onboarding without changing the shared multiplayer rules beneath it.

## Starting Vulnerability

Default expectation: the player begins physically vulnerable.

A normal untrained otherworlder should not automatically fight with professional competence.

Starting combat capability depends on Origin and Skills.

### Combat-Experienced Origin

Possible advantages:

- Combat Awareness
- Weapon Familiarity
- Pain Tolerance
- better starting stamina/control

Possible tradeoffs:

- fewer noncombat advantages
- different social background
- trauma or narrative burdens
- weaker magical/system affinity where appropriate

### System-Literate Origin

Possible advantages:

- faster interface understanding
- better information extraction
- earlier Appraisal/System Comprehension

Possible tradeoffs:

- little real combat conditioning
- incorrect assumptions caused by treating reality like a game

### Academic Origin

Possible advantages:

- Research
- language/history bonuses
- Knowledge progression

Possible tradeoffs:

- physical vulnerability

### Survivor / Criminal Origin

Possible advantages:

- Deception
- Stealth
- Street Sense
- threat awareness

Possible tradeoffs:

- faction prejudice or criminal associations

### Chosen Champion

Possible advantages:

- visible divine Skill or Blessing

Possible tradeoffs:

- obligations
- enemies
- faction expectations
- divine scrutiny

## Starting Skill Choice

Not every Origin should use the same character-creation interface.

Possible Threshold interactions:

- “Choose one Gift.”
- “Allocation available: 3 points.”
- no choice; the system assigns something based on the character
- answer a personal question that indirectly determines a Skill
- accept a bargain with an explicit cost
- reject all offered gifts
- receive a hidden or unknown Skill

This makes character creation itself part of the narrative and reinforces that different entities interpret/administer the system differently.

## First 30 Minutes — Shared Tutorial Spine

Origins may present different scenarios, but the opening should teach the same essential systems.

The player should learn to:

1. move and control the camera
2. interact with the environment
3. understand that physical danger matters
4. use a weapon and basic Weapon Arts
5. encounter or acquire a Skill
6. survive one meaningful threat
7. reach civilization, shelter, or another stable objective
8. realize that their current understanding of the world is incomplete

The opening should use a deliberately limited HUD and avoid front-loading every progression system.

## Origin-Dependent Openings

### Summoned

Ritual chamber → explanation → equipment → political/social obligations.

### Reincarnated

Memory/context sequence → ordinary life or awakening → gradual Skill perception → unusual manifestation.

### Accidental Transfer

Wilderness/ruins → immediate survival → improvised weapon → first safe settlement.

### Failed Hero Summoning

Ritual failure/discard → escape or recovery → discovery that the intended heroes were treated differently.

### Divine Bargain

Threshold negotiation → explicit Skill choice → arrival with known cost or mission.

Different openings should converge toward compatible world progression without erasing their persistent narrative consequences.

## Multiplayer Compatibility

Players with different Origins may perceive the same combat/world information differently.

The authoritative simulation remains shared.

Origin-specific UI should primarily alter:

- terminology
- information precision
- available narrative interpretations
- personal system messages
- hidden-data access

A visitor can therefore enter another host's world while retaining their own character's perception lens.

## Prototype Requirements

The proof of concept should test at least two perception profiles:

1. explicit game-system perception
2. low-information intuitive perception

Both should interact with the same underlying character, enemy, Skill, and manifestation data.

The opening prototype should also test:

- limited initial HUD
- one starting Skill
- one Weapon Art package
- one first-manifestation reveal
- UI expansion after the reveal
- an Origin-specific narrative/system message

## Locked Principles

1. Origins change history, starting capability, narrative access, and system perception.
2. The Threshold exists structurally but can appear radically different between Origins.
3. Skills are broader persistent capabilities, not synonymous with cards.
4. Skills can unlock Weapon Arts, manifestations, and class qualifications.
5. Skill acquisition should primarily come from behavior, training, discovery, and meaningful conditions.
6. System information itself can be progression.
7. The initial HUD should be deliberately limited.
8. The Divine Codex should reveal itself through play rather than being fully exposed immediately.
9. The timing and interpretation of Codex discovery can vary by Origin.
10. Default starting characters are vulnerable unless their background plausibly grants relevant experience.
11. Origin advantages require meaningful tradeoffs or different opportunity costs.
12. Different perception lenses must remain compatible with the same multiplayer simulation.
