# Divine Codex & Isekai Lore Framework

Version: **v0.2.6**

## Core Lore Principle

**Cards are manifestations of the soul's permitted interactions with the laws of the world.**

They are not normally literal physical cards. The card interface is a readable representation of abilities, permissions, memories, techniques, spells, blessings, and higher-order supernatural concepts that a soul can currently manifest.

A character may know far more abilities than can be actively manifested at once. The equipped deck represents the soul's currently attuned **Active Codex**.

## The Transference

When an otherworlder is transported, reincarnated, summoned, or otherwise transferred into the setting, their soul passes through a metaphysical process known provisionally as **the Transference**.

The Transference may involve:

- soul identification
- origin-world recognition
- memory preservation or loss
- compatibility testing
- vessel selection or creation
- divine observation
- patronage offers
- Origin assignment
- Codex attunement
- initial permissions or restrictions

The Transference is not fully understood by mortals, and even the gods may not possess complete authority over it.

## The Divine Codex

**Divine Codex** is the current working term for the soul-bound system through which abilities are represented and accessed.

The Codex contains several conceptual layers:

- **Known Abilities** — techniques, spells, passives, and concepts the character has learned or acquired.
- **Active Codex** — the subset currently attuned for reliable manifestation during danger.
- **Manifested Hand** — abilities currently accessible in the moment.
- **Memory / Return State** — invoked abilities returning to circulation.
- **Exhausted State** — abilities temporarily unavailable because the soul cannot safely invoke them again during the current encounter.

The exact visible terminology may differ between UI and in-world cultures.

## Card-Zone Lore Mapping

| Gameplay Zone | In-World Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Deck | Dormant possibilities within the Active Codex |
| Draw | Manifestation / Awakening |
| Hand | Currently manifested possibilities |
| Play | Invocation / Expression |
| Discard | Memory / Return state |
| Exhaust | Soul exhaustion / temporary lockout |
| Prepared | Deliberately held manifestation |
| Sealed | Restricted or forbidden access |
| In Play | Sustained manifestation |

These terms explain card behavior without requiring characters to physically handle cards in combat.

## Why Deck Size Exists

A mortal soul cannot safely maintain unlimited abilities in a ready-to-manifest state.

The Active Codex therefore acts as an attunement limit.

A character may know dozens or hundreds of abilities while only a smaller set can be stabilized for immediate use.

Deck construction represents **soul attunement**, not inventory management.

This provides an in-world explanation for:

- minimum/maximum deck rules
- card preparation
- specialization
- respec/training systems
- temporary run cards
- sealed abilities
- mastery increasing reliability

## Why Drawing Exists

The soul does not provide perfect simultaneous conscious access to every attuned ability.

Manifestation cycles through the Codex according to metaphysical limitations, mental state, fate, combat pressure, divine influence, and other hidden rules.

This is why a character may know an ability but not have it immediately available.

The player experiences this process as drawing cards.

## Native vs Otherworlder Perception

### Core Direction

The underlying supernatural system is shared, but perception is not necessarily universal.

Otherworlders are especially capable of perceiving the Codex as an explicit structured interface because the Transference translates metaphysical systems into concepts their minds can understand.

A transported person familiar with games may perceive:

- cards
- costs
- rarity
- tags
- status icons
- system messages
- mastery prompts

A native warrior may experience the same process as instinct, discipline, spiritual readiness, or named forms rather than a literal card UI.

A native mage may describe a manifested Spell card as "the next form of the rite becoming available."

Mechanically identical abilities can therefore be understood differently by different cultures and characters.

## Mortal Terminology

Different groups may use different names for the same underlying system.

Possible terms include:

- Skill Cards
- Manifestations
- Soul Imprints
- Divine Script
- Codex Arts
- Soul Forms
- Fragments
- Invocations

No single mortal culture is required to understand the system correctly.

This allows religion, scholarship, Guild bureaucracy, folklore, and ancient civilizations to interpret the same phenomenon differently.

## The Gods

The gods interact with the Codex and the Transference but are **not confirmed creators of the underlying system**.

They may function as:

- administrators
- patrons
- gatekeepers
- domain authorities
- observers
- exploiters
- custodians
- prisoners of the same rules

Different gods may possess different permissions and knowledge.

Some may claim to have created the system even when that claim is false or incomplete.

## Patronage

Otherworlders may attract, accept, reject, inherit, steal, or unknowingly bear divine influence.

Patronage can affect:

- Origins
- Blessings
- Divine quests
- rare cards
- titles
- narrative options
- class unlocks
- miracles
- world reactions

Patronage should create narrative identity rather than functioning as a mandatory optimization layer.

Characters may also remain unaffiliated, oppose the gods, or acquire powers from non-divine sources.

## Ability Hierarchy

The following is a lore-facing hierarchy rather than a strict rarity ladder.

### Techniques

Abilities mortals can learn through training, instinct, and practice.

### Arts

Refined or supernatural martial/skill expressions that exceed ordinary technique.

### Spells

Structured manipulation of mana, elements, concepts, or magical laws.

### Blessings

Abilities or permissions granted through a higher being, covenant, sacred object, or divine system.

### Miracles

Acts that exceed normal mortal capability and invoke significant supernatural authority.

### Authorities

Permissions to alter, override, or directly interact with fundamental rules of the world.

**Authorities are not merely stronger cards. They operate at a higher rules layer.**

An Authority may modify concepts such as:

- death
- identity
- distance
- memory
- causality
- contracts
- summoning
- time
- fate
- Dungeon Laws

Authorities should be extraordinarily rare and narratively significant.

## Divine and Forbidden Cards

Rare supernatural abilities may originate from:

- gods
- ancient beings
- dungeon cores
- forgotten civilizations
- relics
- monsters
- broken Authorities
- corrupted Codices
- stolen permissions

This supports mechanics such as:

- Divine Cards
- Forbidden Cards
- Corrupted Cards
- Authority Cards
- Unique Skills
- Curses
- Miracles

Their source should matter narratively.

## System Messages

System messages are considered potentially canonical manifestations of the Codex rather than purely non-diegetic UI.

Examples:

**CONDITION SATISFIED**

A hidden evolution may be available because the soul has fulfilled an unknown requirement.

**AUTHORITY RECOGNIZED**

A higher-order permission has interacted with the character.

**MANIFESTATION SEALED**

A card or ability has been metaphysically restricted.

System messages may become less reliable, more cryptic, or more revealing as the player uncovers deeper layers of the world.

## Origins and the Transference

Origins may represent how the Transference altered or classified the player rather than only their pre-world biography.

Origin factors can include:

- previous world
- method of arrival
- memory condition
- body/vessel type
- divine interference
- anomalous compatibility
- soul damage
- forbidden inheritance
- failed summoning
- reincarnation circumstances

An Origin may therefore influence narrative events, Codex behavior, class access, and divine attention.

## Relationship to Skill Mastery

Skill Mastery represents the soul becoming increasingly capable of expressing a manifestation reliably and deeply.

Progression can therefore change not only numbers but the **meaning and form** of an ability.

Example:

`Brace` may begin as a mundane defensive technique.

Through repeated protection of allies and specific mastery conditions it may evolve into:

`Guardian's Oath`

At that stage the ability may represent a supernatural vow recognized by the Codex rather than merely improved blocking technique.

## Relationship to Equipment

Certain equipment can contain, teach, amplify, or unlock manifestations.

A weapon may carry:

- stored techniques
- ancestral imprints
- divine blessings
- monster memories
- sealed permissions
- partial Authorities

Equipment Mastery can represent the user's soul becoming synchronized with the item's embedded concepts.

## Relationship to the Endless Dungeon

### Major Narrative Mystery

The Endless Dungeon, Dungeon Laws, the Divine Codex, Authorities, monster evolution, and the Transference are suspected to be connected manifestations of a deeper metaphysical infrastructure.

This is intentionally not fully explained in the early game.

Possible progression of player understanding:

1. **Early:** The gods brought otherworlders here and granted the card-like system.
2. **Midgame:** Different gods appear to possess different levels of control over it.
3. **Late:** Evidence suggests the system predates the currently known gods.
4. **Endgame:** The Endless Dungeon may be part of the same ancient architecture that governs souls, manifestations, and world laws.
5. **Deep Endgame:** The true reason souls cross between worlds becomes a central mystery.

The answers should be revealed through gameplay, factions, ancient ruins, divine conflict, rare Authorities, and deep dungeon discoveries rather than exposition alone.

## Narrative Design Rule

**Game mechanics should increasingly become world lore as the player gains knowledge.**

Systems that initially appear to be convenient game abstractions may later receive in-world explanations.

This includes:

- cards
- deck limits
- rarity
- skill evolution
- hidden classes
- status effects
- Dungeon Laws
- system messages
- respawn/recovery rules where applicable
- divine permissions

## Canon Locked in v0.2.6

The following are now core setting assumptions:

1. Cards are soul manifestations, not normally physical cards.
2. The equipped deck is the character's Active Codex.
3. Drawing represents abilities becoming manifested and immediately accessible.
4. Otherworlders may perceive the system as an explicit game-like interface because the Transference translates it for their minds.
5. Native inhabitants can participate in the same underlying system without necessarily perceiving literal cards.
6. Gods interact with and influence the system but are not confirmed to have created it.
7. Blessings, Miracles, and Authorities represent progressively deeper interaction with supernatural/world rules.
8. Authorities operate at a higher rules layer and are narratively exceptional.
9. The Divine Codex, Transference, Dungeon Laws, and Endless Dungeon are connected to a larger unresolved mystery.
10. The long-form narrative should eventually question why souls are transferred between worlds at all.
