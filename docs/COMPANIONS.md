# Companions & Party Framework

Version: **v0.2.0**

## Purpose

Companions bring the grounded party dependence of early adventuring into the deckbuilding system without forcing the player to manage several full-sized decks at once.

## Core Rule: Main Deck + Support Decks

The player character owns the primary combat deck.

Companions contribute smaller **Support Decks** representing signature skills, reactions, and tactical assistance.

### Prototype Default

- Main Character Deck: 15–25 cards
- Companion Support Deck: 4–8 cards
- Active Companions: 0–2 for the first prototype

Exact values remain subject to playtesting.

## Companion Structure

A companion may have:

- identity and personality
- role tendencies
- class or discipline
- compact Support Deck
- equipment
- loyalty/relationship progression
- personal quest
- unique passive
- injury state
- recruitment requirements

## Party Roles

Roles are descriptive, not hard restrictions.

Potential roles:

- Vanguard
- Defender
- Striker
- Healer
- Controller
- Support
- Scout
- Summoner

A companion may cover multiple roles depending on their card loadout.

## Support Cards

Support Cards enter combat through one of several models:

- drawn into a separate Companion Hand
- triggered automatically under explicit conditions
- activated by spending a shared resource
- inserted into the main deck temporarily

**Prototype Direction:** Use a small visible Companion Hand or cooldown row rather than shuffling all companion cards into the player's deck. This preserves deck identity and makes party planning readable.

## Companion Intent

Companions should telegraph intended autonomous actions similarly to enemies when the player does not control them directly.

Player abilities may:

- redirect companion intent
- empower an action
- cancel an action
- order a specific support skill

## Synergy

Companions should interact with the player's build through tags and mechanics.

Examples:

- a Defender reacts when the player is targeted
- a Healer gains resources when the player spends Health
- a Rogue exploits Stagger created by the player
- a Summoner buffs allied Summons regardless of source

## Companion Progression

Companions may develop through:

- expedition participation
- personal quests
- relationship milestones
- training
- equipment mastery
- major story events

Progression should unlock new support choices rather than simply inflate stats.

## Recruitment

Recruitment sources may include:

- Tavern
- Guild
- rescued NPCs
- faction reputation
- dungeon events
- hidden quests
- defeated rivals
- summoned or constructed allies

## Injury and Defeat

Companions can be injured during failed expeditions.

Normal play should not permanently kill a developed companion without explicit warning and player opt-in.

Possible injury consequences:

- unavailable for several expeditions
- reduced Support Deck size
- disabled signature card
- treatment cost
- personal recovery event

## Solo Builds

Companions are a build option, not a mandatory tax.

Solo characters may gain access to:

- Lone Wolf passives
- additional Treasure capacity
- alternate resource generation
- specialized solo classes or titles

This keeps party and solo play both mechanically valid.

## Summons vs Companions

- **Companion** — persistent character with progression outside combat.
- **Summon** — combat entity created by a card, skill, equipment effect, or class mechanic.

Special content may blur this line deliberately, but the default terminology should remain distinct.

## Design Principle

**Companions should expand tactical possibility without turning every turn into managing three complete characters.**
