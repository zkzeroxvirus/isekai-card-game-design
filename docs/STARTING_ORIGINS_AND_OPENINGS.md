# Starting Origins & Opening Scenarios

**Design Version: v0.2.12**

## Purpose

This document turns the Origin framework into a playable starting package for the first hours of the game.

The goal is not to create six separate games. Each Origin should teach the same foundational controls and converge into the shared world, while meaningfully changing:

- starting Skills
- starting vulnerability
- Threshold presentation
- initial equipment and social context
- System Comprehension
- Codex reveal timing
- faction relationships
- narrative opportunities
- the player's interpretation of progression

## Core Rule

**Every Origin teaches the same world through a different lens.**

Origins should change the player's first assumptions about the setting without requiring incompatible multiplayer rules.

## Launch Origin Set

The initial target is five broadly available Origins plus one later-unlocked anomaly Origin.

### 1. Accidental Transfer — The Stranded

**Fantasy:** You were not invited, chosen, or prepared. You simply arrived.

#### Threshold

Fragmented and almost absent.

The player experiences flashes of geometric structures, distant voices, and a final unreadable confirmation before waking somewhere dangerous.

They do not receive a clear explanation of Skills or the world.

#### Opening Scenario

- wake in wilderness, ruins, or a roadside disaster zone
- minimal HUD
- obtain an improvised weapon
- learn movement, interaction, sprinting, dodge, and basic Weapon Arts through survival
- encounter a weak but genuinely dangerous creature
- first Skill recognition is likely behavior-driven
- reach a road, camp, caravan, or settlement

#### Starting Skill Choice

Choose one practical survival inclination:

- **Quick Learner I** — slightly faster early proficiency recognition from meaningful use
- **Street Sense I** — improved detection of immediate environmental/social danger
- **Endurance I** — improved fatigue tolerance and recovery

#### Starting System Comprehension

Low.

The HUD begins with qualitative health and danger feedback rather than detailed numerical information.

#### First Codex Reveal

Usually reactive and dramatic.

Example trigger: survive an attack that would otherwise overwhelm the character, causing the first manifestation to appear as **Desperate Parry**, **Instinctive Step**, or another survival-linked manifestation.

#### Long-Term Hooks

- unusual Transference investigation
- no obvious patron or summoner
- flexible faction identity
- certain scholars and divine entities become interested in the unexplained arrival

#### Tradeoff

Weakest institutional start, but highest early freedom.

---

### 2. Ritual Summon — The Invited

**Fantasy:** Someone in this world intentionally brought you here.

#### Threshold

Brief but comparatively orderly.

A representative, god, intermediary, or ritual function confirms that a destination has been selected. The exact entity may depend on the faction conducting the summoning.

#### Opening Scenario

- awaken inside a summoning chamber
- immediate NPC witnesses
- receive basic clothing, equipment, and explanation
- undergo physical/Skill evaluation
- learn a weapon package in a controlled training space
- participate in a small field exercise or security incident
- become entangled in the summoning faction's expectations

#### Starting Skill Choice

Choose one sanctioned aptitude:

- **Weapon Familiarity I** — begin with one selected weapon family at basic trained competence
- **Mana Sensitivity I** — perceive mana and magical disturbances earlier
- **Diplomatic Etiquette I** — improved formal social options and institutional interpretation

#### Starting System Comprehension

Moderate.

The player receives named Skills and clearer status feedback early, but does not yet understand the full Active Codex.

#### First Codex Reveal

The faction expects a blessing or supernatural capability, but the player's first manifestation may differ from what the summoners predicted.

This creates an early question: did the ritual actually work correctly?

#### Long-Term Hooks

- obligations to the summoning faction
- political expectations
- possible legal or religious status
- rivals or additional summoned individuals
- access to records about Transference

#### Tradeoff

Strongest support network, lowest initial freedom.

---

### 3. Reincarnated — The Second Life

**Fantasy:** You did not simply arrive. You have already become part of this world.

#### Threshold

Remembered as a dream, childhood memory, recurring image, or something that becomes clearer later.

The player may not initially realize that the memory was real.

#### Opening Scenario

- begin with limited local familiarity
- the character already understands basic language and cultural norms
- complete ordinary tasks or local training before danger intrudes
- the first combat encounter exposes the difference between remembered Earth instincts and the current body's capabilities
- Skill progression initially appears through native terminology rather than explicit game UI

#### Starting Skill Choice

Choose one life-path foundation:

- **Local Lore I** — improved knowledge of common customs, geography, and institutions
- **Craft Discipline I** — choose Cooking, Smithing, Medicine, or another ordinary trade foundation
- **Body Familiarity I** — improved physical control and adaptation to the current vessel

#### Starting System Comprehension

Low to moderate, but culturally integrated.

Instead of visible "XP," the character may initially perceive readiness, familiarity, named forms, or spiritual growth.

#### First Codex Reveal

Usually later than other Origins.

A technique the character believes is merely a learned Skill becomes visibly system-recognized, exposing the distinction between native training and the deeper Codex.

#### Long-Term Hooks

- family or community relationships
- questions about original identity and vessel
- strong native cultural access
- special reactions from people who knew the character before the player fully awakened

#### Tradeoff

Best social integration, but fewer immediate otherworlder advantages and slower recognition of the deeper system.

---

### 4. Failed Hero Summoning — The Rejected

**Fantasy:** You were supposed to be important. The system, ritual, or summoners disagreed.

#### Threshold

Glitched, interrupted, or incomplete.

The player may briefly see another intended destination, another candidate, or an impossible error message before being expelled from the process.

#### Opening Scenario

- wake abandoned, injured, imprisoned, or treated as failed ritual residue
- discover evidence that successful summoned heroes received far better treatment
- escape, recover, or survive with limited equipment
- first combat establishes vulnerability despite supposed "hero" status
- Codex fragments appear inconsistently

#### Starting Skill Choice

Choose one residual imprint:

- **Residual Combat Pattern I** — gain a partially formed combat Skill with unusual growth potential
- **System Echo I** — occasionally perceive hidden system information without understanding it
- **Defiance I** — improved resistance to coercion, fear, or imposed supernatural conditions

#### Starting System Comprehension

Unstable.

The player may see advanced information briefly and then lose access to it.

#### First Codex Reveal

Early, but corrupted or incomplete.

For example, a manifestation appears with missing text, unknown cost information, or an unreadable source field.

#### Long-Term Hooks

- resentment or curiosity toward the successful heroes
- secret records of the failed ritual
- unusual interest from gods/system entities
- possible evidence that failure was deliberate

#### Tradeoff

High mystery and unusual potential, but unstable progression information and poor starting circumstances.

---

### 5. Divine Bargain — The Contracted

**Fantasy:** You remember exactly who—or what—you spoke to before arrival, and you agreed to something.

#### Threshold

The most explicit Threshold experience.

The player meets a defined entity and is offered a bargain rather than a neutral transfer.

The entity's presentation can vary dramatically: benevolent god, transactional administrator, smiling stranger, masked judge, or incomprehensible intelligence translated into a familiar form.

#### Opening Scenario

- Threshold negotiation is part of character creation
- arrive with a known Skill or Blessing
- immediately encounter a situation where the granted power is useful
- learn that the bargain includes a cost, expectation, prohibition, or surveillance condition
- early NPCs may react to divine markers

#### Starting Skill Choice

Choose one Gift from an Origin-specific pool, each paired with an explicit complication.

Examples:

- **Guardian's Instinct I** — improved protection/interception behavior; patron expects defense of designated innocents
- **Appraisal I** — clearer information about objects and creatures; certain hidden entities can also more easily notice you
- **Mana Channeling I** — earlier magical access; physical recovery is initially weaker
- **Fortune's Favor I** — improved unusual-event opportunities; outcomes become less predictable

#### Starting System Comprehension

Moderate to high in the patron's domain.

The character understands some system concepts early but may mistakenly assume their patron controls more than it truly does.

#### First Codex Reveal

Potentially explained in advance, but the first manifestation should still reveal that the Codex exceeds the patron's stated framework.

#### Long-Term Hooks

- patron quests
- divine reputation
- obligations and loopholes
- opportunities to renegotiate, betray, escape, or seize parts of the contract

#### Tradeoff

Strongest explicit supernatural start, but with the clearest attached consequences.

## Later-Unlocked Origin

### 6. System Anomaly — The Unregistered

**Availability:** Intended as a later-unlocked Origin after major Story Revelations.

**Fantasy:** The system does not appear to know what you are.

#### Threshold

No stable form.

The player may see multiple Threshold interpretations simultaneously, inaccessible system structures, or messages not meant for mortal perception.

#### Opening Scenario

- arrival occurs in a location that should be impossible
- NPC/system reactions fail to classify the character correctly
- the player receives partial access to hidden metadata
- ordinary progression rules still function, but several assumptions are visibly broken

#### Starting Skill Choice

No normal choice.

The Origin receives an unknown or sealed Skill whose meaning develops through play.

#### Starting System Comprehension

Paradoxical: high access, low interpretation.

The player sees more data but understands less of it.

#### First Codex Reveal

Immediate but alien.

This Origin is intentionally unsuitable as a first-playthrough default because it reframes central mysteries.

## Background Layer

Origin and previous-world Background should be separate choices where practical.

An Origin answers:

**How did you enter this world?**

A Background answers:

**Who were you before that happened?**

Prototype Backgrounds:

- Civilian / Office Worker
- Student
- Athlete
- Academic / Researcher
- Medical Worker
- Tradesperson
- Soldier / Veteran
- Martial Artist
- Hunter / Outdoorsperson
- Criminal / Street Survivor
- Programmer / Systems Worker
- MMO Player
- Tabletop Player
- Competitive Gamer

Backgrounds should primarily influence starting Skills, perception vocabulary, dialogue, and early competency rather than raw permanent stat superiority.

## Character-Creation Structure

Prototype sequence:

1. Select or discover previous-world Background.
2. Customize appearance/body where appropriate to Origin.
3. Select Origin.
4. Enter Origin-specific Threshold scene.
5. Make the Origin's Skill/Gift decision, if that Origin allows one.
6. Receive hidden starting flags and perception lens.
7. Enter the world through the Origin-specific opening scenario.

Some Origins may reorder these steps for narrative effect.

## Threshold Design Rules

The Threshold should establish mystery without explaining the cosmology too early.

Every Threshold scene should answer at least one question and create at least two more.

Avoid presenting the system as a fully understood character-creation menu unless that interpretation is specific to the player's perception lens.

### Shared Threshold Motifs

Recurring motifs can quietly imply a common infrastructure beneath radically different experiences:

- impossible geometric frames
- distant layered voices
- repeating symbols
- a concept resembling an inventory or ledger
- references to compatibility
- incomplete destination identifiers
- the sensation of being observed by something outside the visible entity

These motifs become meaningful through later Story Revelations.

## First Skill Acquisition Philosophy

The player should receive at least one early Skill recognition within the opening arc, but not every Skill must appear as a dramatic popup.

The first recognized Skill should ideally reflect actual player behavior.

Examples:

- repeatedly checking tracks and environmental clues → **Observation I** or **Tracking I**
- using improvised cover effectively → **Combat Awareness I**
- surviving repeated dangerous exertion → **Endurance I**
- stabilizing an injured NPC → **Medicine I**
- negotiating successfully under pressure → **Negotiation I**

## First Manifestation Package

The first Codex manifestation should come from a small adaptive pool rather than one universal card.

Prototype first-manifestation candidates:

- **Desperate Parry** — defensive response to imminent melee danger
- **Instinctive Step** — emergency reposition/dodge enhancement
- **Focused Strike** — offensive manifestation after repeated disciplined weapon use
- **Protective Impulse** — appears when defending another character
- **Mana Spark** — appears after successful interaction with a magical phenomenon
- **Predator's Mark** — appears after tracking/observing an enemy before combat

The system selects from qualified candidates based on Origin, Background, starting Skill use, and actual behavior.

This means the first card is already telling the player:

**the system noticed what you did.**

## Opening Convergence

Different Origins should converge into a shared regional structure after roughly the first 30–60 minutes.

Convergence does not erase consequences.

By convergence, the player should have:

- basic movement mastery
- one usable weapon family
- at least one persistent Skill
- exposure to meaningful physical danger
- a reason to engage with the Adventurer Guild or another progression institution
- at least one unresolved Origin-specific narrative hook
- either the first manifestation or a clear sign that something deeper exists

## First Shared Region

The prototype shared region should support multiple entry points into the same settlement and surrounding danger zone.

Suggested structure:

- frontier settlement
- nearby forest/road/ruins
- low-rank monster threat
- Adventurer Guild presence
- one religious institution
- one suspicious scholar or researcher
- black-market or criminal presence
- access to the first short dungeon

This region acts as the first comparison point between Origins.

A Summoned character may arrive with official introductions.

An Accidental Transfer may stagger through the gate with no documents.

A Reincarnated character may already know locals.

A Failed Hero may actively avoid authorities.

A Contracted character may attract religious attention.

## Multiplayer Opening Rule

Origin prologues are primarily personal.

Players should normally complete the Threshold and initial tutorial segment before open co-op becomes available.

Once the character reaches the shared regional structure, multiplayer unlocks.

Later replay options may allow experienced players to accelerate or skip portions of an already-completed Origin tutorial while preserving its narrative flags.

## Prototype Origin Matrix

| Origin | Vulnerability | System Clarity | Social Support | Mystery | Early Freedom |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accidental Transfer | High | Low | Very Low | High | Very High |
| Ritual Summon | Medium | Medium | High | Medium | Low |
| Reincarnated | Medium | Low/Medium | Medium/High | Medium | Medium |
| Failed Hero | High | Unstable | Very Low | Very High | High |
| Divine Bargain | Medium | Medium/High | Variable | High | Medium |
| System Anomaly | Variable | Paradoxical | None | Extreme | Extreme |

No row is intended to represent an objectively superior start.

## Prototype Content Target

For the first playable onboarding slice, fully implement only two Origins:

1. **Accidental Transfer — The Stranded**
2. **Ritual Summon — The Invited**

These provide the clearest contrast:

- low-information vs system-aware onboarding
- no support vs institutional support
- survival tutorial vs structured training tutorial
- reactive Codex discovery vs observed/predicted Codex discovery

Both should converge on the same frontier settlement and first dungeon.

After the technical model is proven, add Reincarnated, Failed Hero, and Divine Bargain.

System Anomaly remains later-unlocked content.

## Locked Principles

1. Origin and Background are separate concepts: how you arrived vs who you were before.
2. Initial Origins should be asymmetric without creating an obvious best option.
3. The launch target is five standard Origins plus one later-unlocked anomaly Origin.
4. The Threshold varies by Origin but contains recurring hidden motifs from a common deeper infrastructure.
5. Starting Skills should reflect Origin, Background, and player choices.
6. The first recognized Skill should preferably respond to meaningful player behavior.
7. The first Codex manifestation comes from an adaptive candidate pool rather than one universal tutorial card.
8. Origin prologues teach the same core systems through different narrative and perceptual lenses.
9. Origin paths converge into a shared region without erasing their persistent consequences.
10. Open multiplayer begins after the personal prologue reaches the shared regional structure.
11. The first implementation should fully prototype Accidental Transfer and Ritual Summon before expanding the remaining Origins.
