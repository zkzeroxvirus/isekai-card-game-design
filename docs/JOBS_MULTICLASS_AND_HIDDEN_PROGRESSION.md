# Jobs, Multiclassing & Hidden Progression

**Design Version: v0.2.17**

## Core Principle

**Visible progression tells the player what they can pursue. Hidden progression rewards the person they accidentally became.**

The game should treat character identity as the overlap of Origin, Background, Skills, Jobs, Disciplines, equipment, behavior, faction relationships, world-state consequences, and the Divine Codex.

Multiclassing is therefore not simply selecting two combat classes. It is a system of recognized capabilities, active roles, hidden qualifications, hybrid manifestations, and increasingly unusual classifications.

## Character Identity Layers

The intended progression stack is:

**Origin → Background → Skills ↔ Jobs → Disciplines → Weapon Arts → Codex Manifestations → Hybrid Manifestations → Hidden Jobs / Hidden Disciplines → Authorities**

Each layer answers a different question.

### Origin

How did you enter this world?

### Background

Who were you before Transference?

### Skills

What can you actually do?

### Jobs

What role or identity has the world/system recognized you as fulfilling?

### Disciplines

How do you structure combat capability?

### Weapon Arts

How do training and equipment become reliable combat actions?

### Codex Manifestations

What exceptional abilities can the soul actively manifest?

### Hybrid Manifestations

What new expressions emerge when multiple systems meaningfully overlap?

### Authorities

What permissions allow direct interaction with deeper world laws?

## Jobs

Jobs are persistent identity packages separate from combat Disciplines.

A Discipline primarily answers:

**How do you fight?**

A Job primarily answers:

**What has the world recognized you as capable of being?**

Examples:

- Adventurer
- Explorer
- Hunter
- Herbalist
- Cook
- Blacksmith
- Merchant
- Scholar
- Apothecary
- Scout
- Miner
- Priest
- Gravekeeper
- Beast Handler
- Cartographer
- Smuggler
- Assassin
- Dungeon Delver

Jobs can affect combat indirectly, but they should not become alternate class bars.

## Standard Job Structure

Every substantial Job should be designed using five layers.

### 1. Job Effects

Passive or contextual benefits while the Job is active.

### 2. Job Skills

Skills the Job teaches, improves, or makes easier to discover.

### 3. Job Permissions

Interactions, crafting functions, institutional privileges, tools, or world actions the character can perform because of the Job.

### 4. Job Recognition

How factions, NPCs, institutions, contracts, and narrative events react to the active Job.

### 5. Job Evolutions

Advanced or hidden Jobs that can emerge from mastery, behavior, narrative history, Skills, and unusual combinations.

## Example Job — Hunter

### Effects

- improved interpretation of tracks and field signs
- stronger Bestiary information from observed beasts
- improved harvesting from hunted creatures
- reduced friction in wilderness travel

### Skills

- Tracking
- Fieldcraft
- Beast Lore
- Ranged Familiarity

### Permissions

- advanced hunting/trapping interactions
- more accurate field dressing
- access to hunter contracts and certain wilderness routes

### Recognition

- frontier hunters and wardens react differently
- certain settlements trust demonstrated hunting capability
- monster-related contracts may become available earlier

### Evolution examples

- Master Hunter
- Beaststalker
- Monster Hunter
- Warden of the Wilds

## Example Job — Blacksmith

### Effects

- improved equipment condition assessment
- reduced repair waste
- greater understanding of affixes, materials, and failure states

### Skills

- Smithing
- Metallurgy
- Repair
- Tempering

### Permissions

- use advanced forge interactions
- perform selected equipment modifications
- identify structural weaknesses in gear

### Recognition

- smiths and artisan guilds respond differently
- crafting factions may provide access, contracts, or training

### Evolution examples

- Weaponsmith
- Armorsmith
- Runeforger
- Monster-Smith

## Active Job Slots

Characters may know many Jobs but should not automatically benefit from every Job at full strength simultaneously.

Prototype direction:

- early character: **1 Active Job**
- developed character: **2 Active Jobs**
- advanced character: **3 Active Jobs**
- legendary/system-breaking progression may unlock additional slots through Treasures, rare Origins, hidden qualifications, Authorities, or direct manipulation of the Codex

The exact maximum is deliberately unresolved.

### Design Rule

Active Job slots create build pressure and character identity.

A Guardian with Blacksmith active should feel meaningfully different from a Guardian with Priest, Merchant, Explorer, or Gravekeeper active.

## Job Progression

Job growth should come primarily from relevant activity rather than generic character XP.

Examples:

- Cook progresses through meaningful cooking and ingredient knowledge.
- Explorer progresses through discoveries, route completion, anomaly survival, and expedition work.
- Merchant progresses through trade, contracts, market knowledge, and economic influence.
- Hunter progresses through tracking, hunting, Bestiary observation, and fieldcraft.
- Blacksmith progresses through repair, forging, material use, and equipment evolution work.

### Anti-Grind Rule

Job progression must reward meaningful breadth, difficulty, novelty, accomplishment, and context rather than trivial repetition.

## Job Qualification

Many Jobs should be discovered rather than selected from a universal menu.

Example:

Tracking I + Fieldcraft I + Beast Lore I + multiple successful hunts → **JOB QUALIFICATION DETECTED — HUNTER**

The player may then accept, attune, train, or otherwise formalize the Job depending on context.

Some Jobs can still be taught institutionally. Discovery and formal training are not mutually exclusive.

## Hidden Progression Philosophy

Hidden progression should be one of the defining pleasures of the game.

The world tracks patterns in what the character repeatedly proves about themselves.

**The Codex eventually gives that pattern a name.**

Hidden content can include:

- Jobs
- Disciplines
- Skills
- Titles
- Manifestations
- Hybrid Manifestations
- equipment evolutions
- divine permissions
- Treasures
- narrative states
- Authorities

## Hidden Condition Families

### Behavioral

What the character repeatedly chooses to do.

Examples:

- protecting strangers
- exploiting surrendered enemies
- fighting while critically wounded
- refusing payment
- repeatedly taking trophies

### Mastery

Skills, Jobs, weapons, equipment, Disciplines, and mastery tiers.

### Narrative

Specific choices, relationships, faction histories, promises, betrayals, and quest outcomes.

### World-State

Conditions that can only exist in particular host timelines.

### Equipment

Using, evolving, breaking, refusing, corrupting, restoring, or synchronizing with particular equipment.

### Failure

Defeats, wounds, curses, failed rituals, lost expeditions, companion injuries, and recoveries.

### Combination

Unexpected overlaps between ordinary systems.

### Negative Conditions

Things the player deliberately did not do.

Negative conditions are especially useful for recognizing convictions rather than accumulation.

## Hidden Qualification Design Rule

Hidden unlocks should feel surprising but interpretable in hindsight.

Avoid purely arbitrary secrets.

A player should ideally be able to reconstruct the story of why the Codex recognized them, even if they did not know the exact requirements beforehand.

## Example Hidden Job — Grave Warden

Possible qualifications:

- Gravekeeper Job active or mastered
- Guardian Discipline qualification
- Spirit-related Skill
- defended burial grounds or the dead multiple times
- refused one or more profitable corpse-desecration opportunities
- survived an undead or soul-related dungeon event

The exact recipe can include substitutions so one rigid checklist is not the only route.

## Example Hidden Job — Corpse Broker

Possible qualifications:

- Merchant Job
- Anatomy Skill
- strong Underbridge or criminal ties
- repeated sale or trade of monster/humanoid remains
- negotiated with a necromancer or forbidden researcher
- ignored or opposed Returning Flame restrictions

This should create a materially different social and progression path rather than merely a dark cosmetic title.

## Example Hidden Discipline — Last Bastion

Possible qualifications:

- Guardian mastery threshold
- large number of successful Intercepts
- repeated victories while critically wounded
- never abandoned an incapacitated party member during qualifying events
- deliberately completed major encounters without relying on a healing manifestation

The important requirement is thematic coherence, not the exact numbers.

## Mutual Exclusivity

Some hidden paths should be mutually exclusive within a character history or world timeline.

This prevents completionism from collapsing every character into the same universal checklist build.

Examples:

- an oath requiring lifelong refusal of necromancy may block certain corpse/soul Jobs
- serving one faction through a civil conflict may permanently close an opposing hidden political Job
- accepting a divine covenant may close one route while opening another

### Design Rule

Character history should matter enough that some doors close because other doors were genuinely chosen.

## System Comprehension & Hidden Information

System Comprehension determines how much of hidden progression the character can perceive.

### Low Comprehension

Possible feedback:

> Unknown Qualification Progressed.

or no explicit message at all.

### Developing Comprehension

Possible feedback:

> Hidden qualification conditions satisfied: 3.

The name and requirements remain unknown.

### High Comprehension

Possible feedback:

> Last Bastion — Qualification Progress: 4/5

The identity may be revealed while one or more requirements remain obscured.

### Exceptional Comprehension

The player may see:

- exact missing conditions
- alternate qualification routes
- mutually exclusive conflicts
- hidden provenance
- unusual system classification data

This makes learning how secrets work part of progression.

## Discipline Multiclassing

Characters can qualify for and combine multiple combat Disciplines.

Prototype direction remains **Primary + Secondary Discipline** for the first full combat slice.

Primary and Secondary are not intended to mean 100% power versus 20% power. Both should materially shape the build.

### Multiclass Goals

Multiclassing should create:

- access to multiple manifestation pools
- mixed Weapon Art modifications
- resource interactions
- new Skill qualification routes
- hybrid manifestations
- hidden Job/Discipline opportunities
- new narrative and institutional reactions

It should not simply produce a larger pile of unrelated cards.

## Hybrid Manifestations

Hybrid Manifestations are abilities recognized because two or more compatible identities have become meaningfully integrated.

They should usually require more than simply equipping two Disciplines.

Possible requirements:

- mastery in both Disciplines
- specific Skills
- repeated cross-system behavior
- use of particular weapons
- Jobs
- equipment
- faction/narrative history
- hidden conditions

## Prototype Hybrid Bridges

### Guardian + Duelist

Possible direction: **Blade Warden / Vanguard**

Potential hybrid ideas:

- defensive counters preserve Flow
- Intercept transitions into Passing Cut
- Perfect Guard creates an angle-change opportunity
- protection through aggressive disruption

Example Hybrid Manifestation:

**Crossguard Pursuit**

After Perfect Guarding an attack aimed at an ally, immediately gain a Flow-enabled Passing Cut window against the attacker.

### Arcanist + Duelist

Possible direction: **Spellblade**

Potential hybrid ideas:

- Sigils imbue movement attacks
- elemental weapon states
- Arc Step connects into melee chains
- Marked by Mana enables precision finishers

Example Hybrid Manifestation:

**Runic Passing Cut**

Passing Cut through a compatible Sigil inherits its elemental property and leaves a short-lived rune trace along the movement path.

### Guardian + Arcanist

Possible direction: **Runic Bastion / Arcane Warden**

Potential hybrid ideas:

- defensive wards layered onto physical Guard
- Sigils placed by Intercept endpoints
- magic absorbed through Perfect Guard becomes stored charge
- protective fields that reward physical positioning

Example Hybrid Manifestation:

**Ward of Impact**

Perfect Guarding a compatible magical attack stores its property and allows the next Intercept endpoint to create a compact matching Sigil.

## Jobs as Multiclass Modifiers

Jobs should modify multiclass identity without becoming mandatory recipes.

Examples:

### Guardian + Priest Job

Possible outcomes:

- sacred protection manifestations
- vows
- sanctuary effects
- eventual Templar-like qualification

### Duelist + Assassin Job

Possible outcomes:

- execution windows
- stealth openers
- bleed/opening specialization
- possible Nightblade-style hidden qualification

### Arcanist + Scholar Job

Possible outcomes:

- deeper Sigil analysis
- spell modification
- magical weakness discovery
- Sage-like qualification routes

### Arcanist + Blacksmith Job

Possible outcomes:

- runic equipment
- enchantment
- equipment-anchored Sigils
- Runesmith qualification routes

### Guardian + Arcanist + Blacksmith

Possible hidden direction:

**Runic Bastion**

### Duelist + Arcanist + Hunter

Possible hidden direction:

**Arcane Stalker**

These names remain provisional examples, not guaranteed final classes.

## Shared Build Language

A character description should eventually communicate meaningful identity.

Example:

> Duelist / Arcanist — Hunter + Cartographer

suggests mobile precision, magical setup, field awareness, route knowledge, and wilderness capability.

Another:

> Guardian / Arcanist — Blacksmith + Priest

suggests defense, magical wards, equipment mastery, sacred identity, and possible runic/oath progression.

## Ordinary People vs Otherworlders

Most native people may operate within stricter Job conventions, fewer active Job slots, culturally fixed training routes, or less explicit awareness of qualification rules.

Otherworlders are not automatically stronger, but the Divine Codex may make them unusually capable of:

- perceiving Jobs explicitly
- retaining more Job Imprints
- swapping active Jobs more flexibly
- combining classifications that local institutions treat as incompatible
- discovering hidden qualification feedback
- eventually manipulating Job slots or recognition rules directly

This should feel systemically unusual without making every native character obsolete.

## Endgame Classification Drift

Late-game Jobs and hidden identities can become increasingly conceptual.

Early examples:

- Miner
- Cook
- Soldier
- Hunter

Midgame examples:

- Monster Tamer
- Relic Seeker
- Runeforger
- Dungeon Surveyor

Late examples:

- Dragonslayer
- Kingmaker
- Saint
- Heretic
- Tyrant
- Soul Shepherd

Endgame examples:

- Gatekeeper
- Worldwalker
- Godslayer
- Keeper of the Dead
- Lawbreaker

At sufficient progression, the player should reasonably ask whether the Codex is still describing a profession or classifying what the character has become.

That ambiguity becomes a bridge toward Authorities.

## Prototype Requirements

The first multiclass prototype should test:

1. Primary + Secondary Discipline.
2. One active Job slot.
3. At least four ordinary Jobs.
4. At least one hidden Job.
5. At least one hidden Skill or manifestation.
6. Guardian + Duelist hybrid manifestation.
7. Arcanist + Duelist hybrid manifestation.
8. Guardian + Arcanist hybrid manifestation.
9. System Comprehension hiding/revealing qualification progress.
10. One mutually exclusive hidden path.
11. One qualification triggered partly by negative conditions.
12. One Job changing a narrative interaction in Hearthcross.
13. One Job modifying an equipment mastery/evolution route.

## Locked Principles

1. Jobs and combat Disciplines are separate systems.
2. Jobs primarily represent recognized roles, contextual effects, Skills, permissions, and social identity.
3. Characters may know many Jobs but benefit fully from only a limited number of Active Jobs at once.
4. Job progression comes from relevant meaningful activity rather than generic XP.
5. Hidden progression is a foundational reward structure, not rare side content.
6. Hidden unlocks should be surprising but interpretable in hindsight.
7. Behavioral, mastery, narrative, world-state, equipment, failure, combination, and negative conditions can all contribute to hidden qualifications.
8. Some hidden paths are mutually exclusive so character history remains meaningful.
9. System Comprehension controls how much hidden qualification information is visible.
10. Multiclassing should create integrated identities rather than larger disconnected card piles.
11. Hybrid Manifestations require meaningful cross-system integration, not merely equipping two Disciplines.
12. Jobs can influence hybrid and hidden qualifications without becoming mandatory universal recipes.
13. Otherworlders may be unusually flexible in Job perception and attunement, but natives remain viable and potentially exceptional.
14. Late-game Job classifications can blur into Titles, metaphysical identities, and eventually Authorities.
