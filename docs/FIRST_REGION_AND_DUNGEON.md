# First Region & Dungeon

**Design Version: v0.2.13**

## Purpose

This document defines the first shared region reached after Origin-specific prologues. It is the convergence point for early single-player onboarding and the first place where open co-op becomes available.

The region must prove that the project works as one game: grounded survival, weapon-driven combat, Skills, the Adventurer Guild, faction pressure, living-world consequences, Divine Codex discovery, and a dungeon that hints at the deeper metaphysical mystery.

## Core Principle

**The first region should look ordinary enough that its impossible details matter.**

The player should initially believe they have reached a believable frontier settlement dealing with believable frontier problems. The first dungeon then introduces evidence that the world is stranger than the local explanations suggest.

# The Shared Region

Working regional name: **The Greyfen March**.

Greyfen is a frontier district built around an old trade road, a river crossing, farmland reclaimed from monster territory, and several ruins whose age is disputed.

The region is deliberately compact for the first playable slice. It should contain enough systemic variety to support repeated visits without requiring an open-world-scale landmass.

## Regional Structure

- frontier settlement: **Hearthcross**
- surrounding road network
- Greyfen woodland
- abandoned farms and watch posts
- river crossing and marsh edge
- old quarry/ruin entrance
- first dungeon: **The Buried Gate**
- several small event sites that can change over time

The player should be able to understand the broad geography quickly.

# Hearthcross

Hearthcross is a practical frontier town rather than a grand fantasy capital.

It exists because three things meet here:

1. a trade road
2. a Guild-supported monster frontier
3. access to old ruins and dungeon resources

The settlement should feel functional, slightly strained, and capable of visibly changing over the campaign.

## Initial Visual Identity

- timber, fieldstone, patched plaster, iron hardware
- repaired walls rather than monumental fortifications
- muddy trade lanes and compact market streets
- visible Guild contract boards
- adventurer traffic mixed with ordinary farmers, merchants, laborers, and refugees
- old stonework beneath newer construction, hinting that Hearthcross was built over something older

## Core Locations

### Adventurer Guild Hall

The primary early progression institution.

Functions:

- registration
- low-rank contracts
- party formation
- monster reports
- certification/training referrals
- secured reward processing
- early Bestiary information

The Guild should be useful without being omniscient or morally authoritative.

### The Split Anvil

Blacksmith and equipment-repair workshop.

Introduces:

- repairs
- weapon families
- equipment condition
- basic mastery discussion
- first visible equipment upgrade/evolution hooks

### Lantern House

Inn, meal hall, and social hub.

Introduces:

- rest
- rumors
- recruitable NPCs/companions
- travelers
- regional event gossip
- informal faction information

### Shrine of the Returning Flame

A local religious institution focused on safe journeys, burial, recovery, and return.

It gives the region a sympathetic religious presence without establishing that its theology is objectively correct.

Potential relevance:

- divine reactions to Contracted characters
- death/soul discussions
- healing/recovery
- conflict over strange dungeon artifacts

### Survey Office / Archive Annex

A small scholar-cartographer outpost studying ruins, monster movement, and dungeon expansion.

Key early NPC archetype: a researcher who notices inconsistencies in the player's Codex/Origin or in the age of the dungeon structures.

### Underbridge Market

A semi-legal market beneath/around the old river bridge.

Functions:

- contraband
- stolen monster materials
- rumors
- alternative contracts
- criminal contacts
- early villain/pragmatist options

It should not immediately be presented as a pure evil faction.

# First Regional Factions

The opening region should introduce a manageable subset of the broader faction system.

## Hearthcross Adventurer Guild

Wants:

- safe roads
- controlled monster populations
- reliable dungeon access
- competent adventurers

Conflict:

Its economic incentives may favor keeping the frontier dangerous enough to sustain contracts and resource extraction.

## March Wardens

Local civil/military authority.

Wants:

- order
- tax revenue
- defensible settlements
- controlled migration

Can clash with the Guild over jurisdiction and with refugees/outsiders over security.

## Returning Flame Clergy

Wants:

- protection of souls and burial rites
- containment of spiritually dangerous artifacts
- religious influence

Some members may interpret Codex phenomena as divine; others may privately doubt that explanation.

## Fenroad Consortium

Merchants, caravan owners, and resource buyers.

Wants:

- open roads
- stable prices
- dungeon materials
- predictable security

Can support prosperity while also exploiting frontier labor or monster-resource extraction.

## Underbridge Network

Loose smugglers, fixers, scavengers, and unlicensed adventurers.

Wants:

- access outside Guild control
- profitable secrets
- freedom from March Warden enforcement

Can provide legitimate alternatives as well as criminal opportunities.

# Origin Convergence

Every standard Origin reaches Hearthcross differently.

### The Stranded

Enters through the outer road or refugee gate with little documentation and must prove usefulness or find sponsorship.

### The Invited

Arrives with letters, escorts, or institutional recognition but carries political expectations.

### The Second Life

May already know the town, region, or residents through current-life history.

### The Rejected

May enter under a false identity, avoid official registration, or use Underbridge contacts.

### The Contracted

May attract immediate interest from the shrine or another religious observer.

These routes converge mechanically while preserving narrative flags.

# Early Regional Activity Loop

The first hours after arrival should offer several low-rank problems rather than one mandatory quest corridor.

Prototype activities:

- clear beasts from a farm road
- escort a supply cart
- investigate missing livestock
- recover a Guild survey pack
- help or exploit refugees
- investigate illegal ruin scavenging
- gather medicinal marsh plants
- track goblin scouts
- settle a merchant/Warden dispute
- investigate a strange magical pulse near the quarry

At least three contracts should interact with one another through world state.

Example:

Ignoring goblin scouts can strengthen the later dungeon encounter.

Helping refugees may create new town labor, strain supplies, or open a future district.

Suppressing smugglers may improve Warden reputation while making rare early supplies harder to obtain.

# First Enemy Families

## Greyfen Goblins

The primary intelligent low-tier enemy family.

Design goals:

- individually dangerous to an inexperienced character
- use simple cooperation and ambush tactics
- not automatically cosmically evil
- capable of later becoming allies, rivals, organized factions, or mutated dungeon populations

Prototype roles:

- Forager
- Skirmisher
- Slingrunner
- Brute
- Totem-Caller

## Mirehounds

Predatory beasts used to teach readable attack telegraphs, pack pressure, and positioning.

## Hollowed Vermin

Creatures altered by dungeon leakage.

They should visually hint that the first dungeon can change nearby ecology.

# First Dungeon — The Buried Gate

The Buried Gate is a recently reopened subterranean complex beneath an older quarry/fortification.

Locals believe it is an ancient defensive ruin that has become a monster nest.

That explanation is partially correct and deeply incomplete.

## Dungeon Fantasy

Early layers:

- collapsed masonry
- mining supports
- goblin scavenging camps
- flooded corridors
- old defensive chambers

Deeper layers:

- architecture no local culture can identify
- stone that does not weather correctly
- repeating geometric seams
- doors that respond to Skills/Codex states
- impossible measurements
- faint system-like symbols visible only to some characters

The dungeon should transition visually from grounded ruin to restrained metaphysical anomaly.

# First Dungeon Structure

Target first-run length: approximately 25–40 minutes after players know basic controls.

Prototype sequence:

1. **Quarry Approach** — optional exterior event and route choice
2. **Broken Entry Hall** — first coordinated combat
3. **Scavenger Camp** — goblin encounter with optional negotiation/observation path
4. **Flooded Works** — traversal/hazard room
5. **Old Guard Chamber** — rest/checkpoint and first deeper clue
6. **Split Route** — combat-heavy route vs exploration/puzzle route
7. **The False Wall** — revelation that the known ruin sits against something older
8. **Gate Antechamber** — elite encounter
9. **The Buried Gate** — first boss and Story Revelation
10. **Extraction Chamber** — secured reward/checkpoint and return route

# Dungeon Teaching Goals

The first dungeon should teach:

- party spacing
- Weapon Arts under sustained combat
- active hand cycling
- one simple cross-player status interaction
- hazards
- secured vs unsecured loot
- route choice
- Bestiary observation
- Skill checks outside combat
- environmental storytelling
- the difference between ordinary ruins and Dungeon architecture

# First Boss — Gatebound Hob

Working boss name: **Gatebound Hob**.

A goblin leader has been changed by prolonged exposure to the deeper structure.

The fight should initially look like an upgraded goblin encounter, then reveal one impossible mechanic.

Prototype phases:

### Phase 1 — Warlord

- shield/cleaver combat
- commands goblin reinforcements
- obvious interrupt windows
- teaches target prioritization

### Phase 2 — Gate Resonance

At low health or after a room interaction, the buried structure activates.

- geometric marks appear across the boss
- attacks gain short displacement/distortion effects
- one arena rule changes
- players receive different UI/perception feedback depending on System Comprehension

The goal is not endgame spectacle. It is the first undeniable evidence that dungeon phenomena do not fit ordinary magic explanations.

# First Story Revelation

After the boss, the party reaches the actual Buried Gate.

The Gate should provide different information to different characters.

Possible observations:

- a System-literate player sees an unreadable permission or access classification
- a low-Comprehension player hears a pressure-like tone or experiences recognition without language
- a Contracted player senses their patron react or become silent
- a scholar identifies that the structure predates the civilization supposedly responsible for the ruins

Shared fact:

**The Gate recognizes the party in some way.**

It does not open fully.

This becomes the first Core Mystery Story Revelation.

# First Dungeon Choices

The dungeon should include at least one consequential decision beyond combat.

Prototype decision: the goblin scavenger camp.

Options may include:

- exterminate the camp
- drive them out
- negotiate for passage
- secretly trade
- rescue a captive
- betray an agreement
- investigate why they entered the ruins

Consequences can affect:

- later reinforcements
- Guild report
- Warden reaction
- Underbridge contacts
- future goblin relations
- available information about the boss

There should be no morality meter reward attached to the choice.

# First Region Living-World Seeds

Hearthcross should contain several state variables that can visibly change later.

Prototype seeds:

- refugee population
- road safety
- goblin hostility/organization
- merchant confidence
- Warden control
- Guild influence
- dungeon activity
- shrine influence
- Underbridge strength

These variables can alter NPC presence, prices, contracts, patrols, architecture, dialogue, and later quests.

# Multiplayer Role

Open co-op becomes available once the personal Origin prologue is complete and Hearthcross is reached.

The host's Hearthcross state is authoritative.

Visitors may bring:

- different Origin dialogue
- Skills that unlock alternate dungeon interactions
- different System Comprehension readouts
- faction relationships
- Bestiary/Knowledge information

The first dungeon should therefore contain several optional interaction points that can respond to party composition without requiring a specific build.

# Prototype Scope Guardrails

For the proof of concept, Hearthcross should not be built as a giant city.

Required playable spaces:

- Guild Hall
- one inn/social interior
- blacksmith
- shrine exterior/interior slice
- Underbridge access point
- compact market/street loop
- town gate
- route to quarry/dungeon

The surrounding region can use a small number of reusable encounter sites connected by readable travel paths.

The goal is density and consequence, not acreage.

# Locked Principles

1. Hearthcross is the first shared settlement and multiplayer convergence point.
2. The first region is a compact frontier sandbox, not a large open world.
3. Ordinary frontier problems coexist with subtle evidence of deeper world systems.
4. The first dungeon is The Buried Gate.
5. The dungeon transitions from believable ruin to restrained metaphysical anomaly.
6. The first boss introduces one impossible Dungeon/System mechanic rather than endgame-scale spectacle.
7. The Buried Gate itself provides the first shared Core Mystery Story Revelation.
8. Origin and System Comprehension alter interpretation of the same revelation.
9. At least one dungeon encounter supports negotiation or non-extermination outcomes.
10. Early regional choices seed persistent living-world changes.
11. Hearthcross must remain useful as the player's world grows rather than becoming a disposable tutorial town.
12. Prototype production prioritizes a dense, reactive region over geographic size.
