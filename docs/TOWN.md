# Town & Settlement Progression Framework

Version: **v0.2.21**

## Core Principle

Settlements are persistent places where people live, work, trade, build, argue, worship, govern, and respond to the player's history.

A town is not an abstract upgrade menu.

> **Settlement growth increases depth before breadth.**

A settlement should become better at what it already is rather than eventually containing every possible system in the game.

See [`SOCIETY_ECONOMY_AND_REPUTATION.md`](SOCIETY_ECONOMY_AND_REPUTATION.md) for the broader economy, profession, citizenship, reputation, property, and business rules.

## 1. Settlement Functions

A settlement may support:

- trade
- lodging
- employment
- Jobs and training
- crafting
- professional work
- legal registration
- faction relationships
- property
- construction
- business
- healthcare
- religion
- research
- rumors
- contracts
- companions
- social relationships
- world-state consequences

Not every settlement supports every function.

## 2. Settlement Identity

Every substantial settlement should have a limited identity defined by factors such as:

- geography
- population
- culture
- institutions
- industries
- dominant factions
- trade routes
- local threats
- religion
- laws
- available professions
- resources

These determine which Jobs, goods, services, construction options, and social routes exist locally.

## 3. Settlement State

Prototype settlement variables may include:

- Population
- Security
- Food
- Raw Materials
- Medicine
- Labor
- Housing
- Trade Access
- Prosperity
- Infrastructure
- Faction Control
- Refugee Pressure
- Magical Stability
- Crime / Black-Market Pressure

Only variables that create visible consequences should be tracked.

## 4. Shops and Services

Stores should respond to local conditions rather than function as infinite static inventories.

A business may depend on:

- supply
- demand
- prosperity
- trade access
- owner wealth
- owner relationship
- legality
- faction state
- recipes / production knowledge
- player-supplied materials

A shop can improve because the player opens trade routes, recruits a specialist, supplies rare materials, or funds expansion.

It can decline because of war, shortages, crime, destroyed infrastructure, or lost suppliers.

## 5. Employment and Professions

Settlements provide work appropriate to their economy and culture.

Examples:

- caravan labor
- workshop assistance
- guard work
- medicine
- field labor
- hunting
- messenger work
- surveying
- construction
- cooking
- trade
- criminal logistics

Work can contribute to:

- money
- Skill progress
- Job qualification
- reputation
- relationships
- rumors
- hidden progression

Ordinary work should occasionally intersect with larger adventures.

## 6. Citizenship and Residency

Settlements may recognize statuses such as:

- visitor
- registered traveler
- temporary resident
- permanent resident
- citizen / subject
- licensed professional
- landholder
- business owner

Legal status can affect:

- property rights
- business access
- taxes
- licenses
- guild membership
- legal protection
- political participation
- restricted training
- weapon rights
- local Jobs

Different cultures use different rules.

## 7. Property

Property can include:

- rented room
- apartment
- workshop
- home
- shop
- forge
- warehouse
- farm
- frontier claim
- estate

Property can provide storage, crafting access, business functions, customization, social scenes, local status, and construction opportunities.

Ownership depends on law, wealth, reputation, land availability, and world state.

## 8. Construction

Construction uses defined plots, renovations, civic projects, district projects, or frontier claims by default.

Projects can require:

- land / permission
- plans
- materials
- labor
- specialists
- money
- time
- security

Player Skills and Jobs can let the character directly perform parts of the work or improve quality and efficiency.

Construction should visibly change the settlement when feasible.

## 9. Settlement Projects

Projects can be initiated by:

- local government
- factions
- guilds
- businesses
- religious groups
- communities
- the player

Examples:

- palisade repair
- road improvement
- clinic expansion
- refugee housing
- new warehouse
- market expansion
- shrine restoration
- bridge construction
- black-market tunnel
- guild annex

Projects can affect economy, population, Jobs, security, faction influence, property values, and future opportunities.

## 10. Businesses

Advanced progression may allow ownership or operation of businesses.

Examples:

- forge
- tavern
- clinic
- shop
- warehouse
- caravan company
- alchemy practice
- farm
- expedition company

Business ownership should involve meaningful choices but support delegation so it does not become mandatory spreadsheet management.

Possible decisions include:

- suppliers
- workers
- specialization
- contracts
- faction alignment
- expansion
- security
- legal versus illicit activity

## 11. Building Upgrade Philosophy

A building upgrade should preferably:

1. unlock a new action,
2. deepen a local profession,
3. expand meaningful choice,
4. improve information,
5. enable new content,
6. alter production or services,
7. visibly change the location,
8. reduce friction on an already-understood system.

Flat percentage bonuses may exist but should not define settlement progression.

## 12. Hearthcross Example

Hearthcross is a frontier settlement with deliberately limited breadth.

Core institutions include:

- Adventurer Guild Hall
- Split Anvil
- Lantern House
- Survey Office / Archive Annex
- Shrine of the Returning Flame
- Warden checkpoint
- Underbridge access

Possible development directions include:

### Guild Expedition Desk

Deepens contracts, expedition support, adventuring employment, and local logistics.

### Split Anvil Expansion

Deepens frontier repair, material handling, equipment production, and Smith progression.

### Lantern House / Clinic Expansion

Deepens lodging, medicine, recovery, food, and Field Medic opportunities.

### Survey Annex

Deepens mapping, dungeon research, regional knowledge, and Surveyor progression.

### Warden Infrastructure

Deepens road safety, patrol capacity, legal enforcement, and Scout/Warden opportunities.

### Underbridge Network

Deepens illicit trade, smuggling, fencing, hidden Jobs, and black-market access.

Hearthcross should not become a magical academy, royal court, great temple complex, master artisan metropolis, and international trading capital simultaneously.

## 13. Town Resources

Avoid adding many bespoke town currencies.

Prefer shared world resources such as:

- normal money
- construction materials
- actual crafted goods
- labor availability
- faction contributions
- specific rare materials

Abstract project values may exist internally, but the player should not need to manage a dozen settlement currencies.

## 14. Dynamic Growth

Settlement development can depend on:

- economic state
- world time
- trade routes
- population
- NPC survival/recruitment
- player investment
- faction control
- construction
- war
- crimes
- monster diplomacy
- major discoveries

Growth may also reverse.

Buildings can close, burn, change ownership, become corrupted, be repurposed, or recover later.

## 15. Multiplayer

Settlement state belongs to the host timeline.

Visitors experience the host's shops, shortages, construction, local NPC memories, laws, and development.

Player-owned portable progression such as Skills, Jobs, and equipment remains personal.

Local property, local businesses, and citizenship do not automatically transfer between host timelines.

## 16. Design Guardrails

- Do not turn settlements into upgrade screens disguised as 3D spaces.
- Do not make every town offer every service.
- Do not add construction systems that require survival-game free placement unless a future feature strongly justifies it.
- Do not make economy management mandatory for combat-focused players.
- Do not let passive business income trivialize exploration and adventuring.
- Do not create excessive town-only currencies.
- Let settlement growth visibly reflect people, resources, politics, and player choices.
- Let Jobs and Skills change how the player participates in civilian systems.

## Locked Principle

> **A settlement should feel like a place the player can belong to, work in, invest in, alter, leave behind, return to, and recognize as changed.**
