# Skill Mastery & Evolution

Version: **v0.2.0**

## Purpose

Skills are not static unlocks. A character should gradually learn, refine, specialize, and sometimes transform abilities through repeated use, training, discoveries, and unusual accomplishments.

This system exists to make a card feel like a learned RPG ability rather than a disposable deckbuilding object.

## Core Rule: Knowledge vs Mastery

A character may **Know** a card without having mastered it.

- **Known Skill** — the character is allowed to equip the card.
- **Mastered Skill** — the character has developed permanent expertise with the skill and may unlock stronger forms, alternate evolutions, or associated passives.

Run-only cards normally do not gain persistent mastery unless explicitly converted into a learned skill by a reward, trainer, class feature, or achievement.

## Skill Mastery Sources

Mastery may be earned through:

- playing the skill in meaningful combat
- fulfilling card-specific conditions
- completing class training
- defeating appropriate enemies
- resolving related class events
- using the skill in a combo or engine
- completing achievements
- studying with an NPC or town facility

Pure repetition should not always be sufficient. Higher mastery tiers should increasingly require demonstrations of understanding rather than raw use count.

## Prototype Mastery Tiers

1. **Learned** — skill is available for deck construction.
2. **Practiced** — minor reliability or utility unlock.
3. **Refined** — first meaningful upgrade branch.
4. **Mastered** — advanced branch, passive, or synergy unlock.
5. **Evolved** — thematic transformation requiring a special condition.

Not every skill must use all five tiers.

## Evolution Branches

A skill may branch into mechanically different evolved forms.

Example:

### Shield Bash

Base:
- Deal damage.
- Gain Guard.

Possible evolutions:

**Crushing Bash**
- increased Stagger
- offense/control identity

**Guardian Bash**
- increased Guard
- Taunt or protection identity

**Runic Bash**
- scales with Mind or magical resources
- enables Knight + Arcanist builds

The choice should alter build direction, not simply offer +10% versus +12% damage.

## Evolution Conditions

Evolution requirements may include:

- skill mastery tier
- class mastery tier
- equipment mastery
- specific attribute threshold
- specific title or achievement
- multiclass combination
- boss kill
- dungeon biome exposure
- monster material
- town trainer
- rare event
- hidden condition

Some conditions may remain undisclosed until discovered.

## Skill Families

Cards may belong to a **Skill Family** representing related techniques.

Example:

Shield Discipline
- Brace
- Shield Bash
- Intercept
- Fortress Stance
- Shield Charge

Mastering multiple cards in a family may unlock:

- family passives
- advanced techniques
- hidden classes
- specialization branches
- equipment evolutions

## Class Mastery Relationship

Skill Mastery and Class Mastery are related but distinct.

- **Skill Mastery** asks: How deeply have you learned this ability?
- **Class Mastery** asks: How deeply have you learned this discipline?

A player may master one signature Knight technique while remaining only moderately experienced with the Knight class overall.

## Use-Based Progression Guardrails

To prevent degenerate grinding:

- trivial encounters may provide reduced or no mastery
- repeated use in one combat may have diminishing credit
- advanced tiers should require achievements or challenge conditions
- mastery should reward correct or thematic use where possible

## Hidden Skill Evolution

Some cards may possess secret evolutions.

Example:

A healing spell repeatedly used while the caster is critically wounded could reveal a blood-sacrifice branch.

A defensive technique used to protect companions from lethal attacks could reveal a guardian branch.

These discoveries should make the world feel as though it contains rules the player can uncover rather than a fully exposed upgrade spreadsheet.

## Content Guideline

Every major class should eventually contain:

- foundational skills
- engine skills
- utility skills
- one or more skill families
- branching evolutions
- at least one hidden or unusual evolution path

## Design Principle

**Using an ability teaches the character what that ability can become.**
