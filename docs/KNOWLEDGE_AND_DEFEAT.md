# Knowledge, Bestiary & Defeat Consequences

Version: **v0.2.0**

## Purpose

The player should feel that learning how the world works is itself a form of permanent progression. Failure should hurt enough to create tension, but it should also produce information and future opportunity rather than erase long-term character investment.

## Knowledge as Persistent Progression

Knowledge may reveal:

- enemy statistics
- behavior patterns
- weaknesses
- resistances
- loot tables
- evolution conditions
- dungeon hazards
- event outcomes
- crafting recipes
- hidden class clues
- boss phase information

Knowledge is stored permanently unless a specific rule states otherwise.

## Bestiary Discovery Levels

Prototype model:

### Unknown
- Name may be hidden.
- HP range unknown.
- Abilities unknown.

### Observed
- Basic identity revealed.
- Approximate HP range.
- Common intent patterns visible.

### Studied
- Weaknesses and resistances revealed.
- Common drops visible.
- Signature abilities documented.

### Researched
- Exact or near-exact combat information.
- Rare drops and evolution variants.
- Hidden behavior notes.
- Crafting and class interactions may become visible.

## Knowledge Sources

Knowledge can be gained through:

- fighting enemies
- surviving enemy abilities
- defeating enemies
- collecting monster materials
- Guild reports
- Library research
- Cartographer discoveries
- NPC dialogue
- class abilities
- scouting
- achievements

Different sources may reveal different kinds of information.

## Monster Knowledge Example

### First Encounter

**Goblin Scout**
- Health: ???
- Weakness: ???
- Behavior: ???

### Observed

**Goblin Scout**
- Health: Low
- Often attacks wounded targets.
- May retreat when isolated.

### Researched

**Goblin Scout**
- Health range visible.
- Weakness and resistance tags visible.
- Prioritizes targets below a Health threshold.
- Rare material: Scout's Lens.
- Killing one before it signals allies can reveal a hidden achievement condition.

## Defeat Philosophy

Defeat should create:

1. **Immediate loss** — the expedition was unsuccessful.
2. **Recovery pressure** — injuries, repairs, or lost unsecured rewards.
3. **Persistent learning** — knowledge, mastery credit, achievements, or new opportunities may still survive.

The goal is tension without character deletion.

## Prototype Defeat Consequences

On normal difficulty, defeat may cause some combination of:

- unsecured dungeon currency lost
- temporary run upgrades lost
- unextracted materials partially lost
- equipment damage
- character Wounds
- companion Injuries
- contract failure
- reputation consequences

Persistent systems normally retained:

- Known Skills
- Skill Mastery already secured
- Class Mastery
- Achievements
- Titles
- Bestiary knowledge
- Town upgrades
- Treasures already extracted
- permanent recipes and discoveries

## Wounds

Wounds are temporary persistent consequences caused by severe damage or defeat.

Examples:

- **Fractured Arm** — penalties to Heavy or two-handed Techniques.
- **Mana Burn** — reduced starting magical resource.
- **Deep Cut** — starts combat with Bleed.
- **Shaken** — reduced effectiveness against Fear effects.

Wounds may be treated through:

- Temple
- Alchemist
- Rest
- specialist NPCs
- consumables
- class abilities

## Secured vs Unsecured Loot

Dungeon rewards may exist in two states:

- **Unsecured** — carried during the expedition and vulnerable to defeat.
- **Secured** — permanently banked through extraction, checkpoint, special container, or explicit effect.

This creates meaningful decisions about whether to push deeper or return with what has already been earned.

## Retreat

Retreat should usually be better than defeat but worse than successful completion.

Possible retreat outcomes:

- keep secured rewards
- retain a percentage of unsecured materials
- preserve equipment condition
- fail current contract objective
- surrender depth bonus or boss reward

## Recovery Runs

Some failed expeditions may create a **Recovery Contract** or **Lost Cache** event.

This can allow the player to revisit the location to reclaim a portion of lost resources, defeat a monster that took the loot, or rescue an injured companion.

Recovery content turns failure into new stories instead of pure punishment.

## Difficulty Modes

Harsher loss rules may exist as opt-in challenge modifiers.

Examples:

- no recovery cache
- larger material loss
- severe wounds
- equipment breakage
- companion permadeath

Permanent character or companion death should never be part of the standard rules without explicit warning.

## Design Principle

**Failure should cost resources and create stories, while knowledge survives and makes the next expedition smarter.**
