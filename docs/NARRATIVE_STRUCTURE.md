# Narrative Structure

Version: **v0.2.8**

## Core Narrative Principle

The game is a **sandbox-first living world with a discoverable core mystery**.

The player is not required to follow a rigid main-quest chain. The world supports independent exploration, faction involvement, dungeon delving, town development, criminal or heroic activity, crafting, companion stories, and personal goals.

The central storyline exists to provide long-form direction and to reveal the deeper truth behind the Divine Codex, Transference, Authorities, Dungeon Laws, and the Endless Dungeon.

## Five Narrative Layers

### 1. Core Mystery

The Core Mystery concerns the deepest truths of the setting:

- why otherworlders are transferred
- what the Divine Codex actually is
- why the Endless Dungeon exists
- what Authorities represent
- how gods interact with the system
- whether the known gods created any part of it
- what larger architecture connects soul transfer, Dungeon Laws, and manifestation

The Core Mystery should be discovered through evidence, anomalies, artifacts, encounters, and revelations rather than a constant linear quest chain.

### 2. Regional Storylines

Each major region should have one or more conflicts that can resolve differently depending on player action, inaction, faction relationships, and world state.

Regional arcs may involve:

- wars
- succession disputes
- monster-human conflict
- religious schisms
- dungeon frontiers
- trade disputes
- famine or resource pressure
- criminal organizations
- magical disasters
- political revolutions
- settlement expansion
- refugee movements
- divine disputes

Regional storylines may advance or resolve without the player.

### 3. Character & Companion Arcs

Important NPCs and companions should have persistent personal stories affected by:

- relationship state
- player behavior
- faction allegiance
- world events
- time
- survival or death of related NPCs
- class or Origin
- knowledge
- equipment or Treasures
- narrative decisions

Companions may approve, object, leave, betray, reconcile, change beliefs, unlock personal classes or cards, or become major political/world actors.

### 4. Emergent World Stories

The Living World system creates narrative through interacting state rather than authored quest chains alone.

Examples:

- a spared monster later becomes a faction leader
- an ignored settlement collapses and creates refugees
- a rescued merchant becomes a major trade figure
- a faction project succeeds because the player was absent
- a companion changes allegiance after repeated ideological conflict
- an old dungeon expedition creates a later political consequence

Emergent stories should make the player's campaign feel authored by their history rather than by a universal script.

### 5. Player Campaign History

The player's actual story is the combination of:

- Core Mystery discoveries
- Regional Storyline outcomes
- Character/Companion arcs
- Emergent World Stories
- faction relationships
- moral/behavioral tendencies
- town development
- dungeon achievements
- Titles and Authorities
- player-created goals

Two characters or host worlds may therefore share the same underlying setting while producing radically different campaign histories.

## Core Story Progression Through Revelations

The central storyline should primarily progress through **Story Revelations** rather than mandatory quest completion.

Possible revelation sources:

- ancient dungeon inscriptions
- contradictory religious texts
- anomalous Codex behavior
- otherworlders with unusual Transference histories
- dead or forgotten gods
- corrupted manifestations
- Authority fragments
- research by scholars
- forbidden faction archives
- companion discoveries
- high-depth Dungeon Laws
- divine encounters
- artifacts that predate accepted history

Revelations may be grouped into thematic chains. When enough compatible evidence has been discovered, new Core Mystery events, locations, conversations, or choices become available.

The player should feel that they are **discovering the plot**, not merely following it.

## No Constant Apocalypse Timer

### Core Rule

The Core Mystery should not punish the player for spending time on ordinary life, exploration, town development, crafting, or regional stories through an invisible global failure countdown.

Some regional crises and faction projects can absolutely advance with time, fail, escalate, or resolve without intervention.

The deepest central mystery should instead advance through:

- World Era
- player discoveries
- major world-state transitions
- faction actions
- character power/knowledge thresholds
- deliberate story engagement

This avoids the contradiction of claiming the world will end tomorrow while allowing the player to spend weeks fishing or crafting.

## Era-Based Narrative Escalation

The provisional Living World eras also structure narrative scope.

### Arrival Era

Primary narrative concerns:

- survival
- understanding Transference
- learning the Codex
- establishing identity
- joining or avoiding institutions
- local NPCs and settlements

The Core Mystery is mostly background noise and isolated anomalies.

### Adventurer Era

Primary narrative concerns:

- regional travel
- Guild and faction relationships
- early dungeon anomalies
- conflicting interpretations of the Codex
- otherworlder encounters
- hidden classes and forbidden manifestations

The player begins finding evidence that accepted explanations are incomplete.

### Heroic Era

Primary narrative concerns:

- large regional conflicts
- major faction projects
- gods or divine agents taking notice
- repeated Codex/System anomalies
- rare Authorities
- deep-dungeon revelations

The Core Mystery becomes difficult to dismiss but remains morally open-ended.

### Sovereign Era

Primary narrative concerns:

- nations and political power
- control of dungeon resources
- religious authority
- faction alliances and wars
- world-level projects
- direct competition over ancient system knowledge

The player's previous relationships materially alter who participates in the Core Story and what options exist.

### Mythic Era

Primary narrative concerns:

- Authorities
- gods
- ancient system architecture
- Transference origins
- world laws
- Endless Dungeon truth
- choices that can alter metaphysical rules

The player may seek to preserve, reform, seize, destroy, escape, or transcend parts of the system.

## Morally Open Core Story

The Core Mystery must not assume that the player is heroic.

Different characters may pursue the truth for different reasons:

- protect the world
- understand their own Transference
- gain power
- overthrow gods
- restore divine authority
- seize control of the system
- free otherworlders
- stop soul transfer
- exploit soul transfer
- preserve the system because removing it is worse

Main-story choices should be judged by concrete consequences, faction reactions, companion responses, and later world states rather than a canonical Good Ending / Evil Ending split.

## Narrative Keys

Character progression should unlock narrative options.

Potential narrative keys include:

- Origin
- Class
- mastered Skill
- equipment mastery
- Treasure
- Authority
- Title
- achievement
- Guild Rank
- faction reputation
- behavioral tendency
- Bestiary Knowledge
- World Knowledge
- companion presence
- prior decision
- world project state
- settlement state
- Codex anomaly

This reinforces the rule that a build is both a combat configuration and a set of ways to understand and interact with the world.

## Regional Arc Design Rule

A regional storyline should ideally support at least three broad approaches rather than a binary choice.

Examples:

- support one side
- support the opposing side
- negotiate or create a third outcome
- exploit the conflict
- overthrow both sides
- refuse involvement and allow the world simulation to resolve it

Not every approach must always be possible. Character capabilities and previous choices may create or remove options.

## Narrative State Model

Narrative content should be driven by explicit state wherever practical.

Useful state categories:

- discovery flags
- NPC alive/dead/missing state
- relationship state
- faction reputation
- faction project stage
- settlement condition
- conflict state
- World Era
- time elapsed
- Story Revelation groups
- player Titles
- class/skill/equipment conditions
- behavioral tendencies
- companion states
- divine patronage/hostility
- Authority ownership or exposure

Narrative events should query these states rather than rely on a single rigid quest sequence.

## Multiplayer Narrative Rule

The host world's timeline determines current narrative reality.

Visiting players may:

- experience regional and Core Mystery events in the host timeline
- contribute combat and exploration
- provide narrative keys based on their visiting character
- gain portable personal rewards where allowed

Visiting players do not automatically import their own world decisions into the host timeline.

A visitor may therefore encounter NPCs, settlements, alliances, enemies, or revelations that differ substantially from their own world.

## Canon Narrative Rules Locked

1. The game has a real Core Mystery.
2. The Core Mystery is discovery-driven rather than a rigid linear quest chain.
3. The world remains worth inhabiting even when the player ignores the Core Mystery.
4. Regional stories can progress independently and resolve without the player.
5. Companion and NPC arcs react to persistent world and relationship state.
6. Emergent world consequences are a first-class source of narrative.
7. Player campaign history is the true personalized story.
8. Main-story morality is open-ended; heroic and villainous characters can both pursue the central truth.
9. There is no permanent global apocalypse timer forcing constant main-quest progression.
10. Narrative scale grows with World Era from personal survival to metaphysical decisions.
11. Character builds provide narrative keys as well as combat capabilities.
12. In multiplayer, the host timeline defines narrative reality.

## Deliberately Unresolved

- exact opening sequence of the game
- identity of the first major Core Mystery revelation
- whether the protagonist is uniquely anomalous
- number and geography of major regions
- exact regional story arcs
- final endgame choices
- whether the Core Mystery has a single objective truth or multiple partially valid interpretations
- whether multiple campaign endings can coexist with continued postgame play
