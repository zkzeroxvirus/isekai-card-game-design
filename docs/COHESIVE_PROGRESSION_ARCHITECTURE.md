# Cohesive Progression Architecture

**Design Version: v0.2.20**

## Purpose

The game has many progression ideas, but the player should not feel as though they are managing a dozen unrelated systems.

The design therefore organizes progression into six player-facing domains:

1. **Character** — Origin, Background, Attributes, Skills
2. **Combat Build** — Weapon, Discipline, Development Grid, Divine Codex
3. **Role** — Jobs
4. **Gear** — Equipment, equipment mastery and evolution
5. **Discovery** — Knowledge, hidden qualifications, System Comprehension
6. **World** — factions, relationships, settlement state, narrative consequences

Every future progression mechanic should belong clearly to one of these domains unless it has a compelling reason to become a new major system.

---

## 1. Character

### Origin

How the character entered the world.

Origin establishes narrative context, starting perception, unusual system interactions, and possible hidden progression routes. Origin is not a combat class.

### Background

Who the character was before Transference.

Background may influence starting Skills, knowledge, dialogue, social assumptions, or believable starting competencies.

### Attributes

Prototype attributes remain:

- Might
- Finesse
- Mind
- Spirit
- Vitality

Attributes should support build direction without overpowering equipment, Discipline choices, Codex synergy, player execution, or discovery.

### Skills

Skills represent persistent capabilities such as:

- Swordsmanship
- Tracking
- Appraisal
- Cooking
- Smithing
- Medicine
- Mana Perception
- Deception
- Fieldcraft
- System Comprehension

Skills primarily improve through meaningful use, training, study, accomplishments, teachers, equipment interactions, and hidden conditions.

**Skills do not use a universal giant skill tree.**

The player-facing rule is:

> Do meaningful things, train them, and become better at them.

Skills may still have mastery tiers, branches, special techniques, or evolutions, but they should share a common mastery language rather than each becoming an independent progression minigame.

---

## 2. Combat Build

Combat consists of four linked parts.

### Foundation Actions

Reliable universal actions such as movement, aim, sprint, dodge, interaction, traversal, and basic target handling.

These never depend on card draw.

### Weapon Arts

Reliable combat actions produced by:

**Weapon Family + Discipline + Equipment Mastery = Weapon Moveset**

Weapon Arts form the dependable action-combat vocabulary.

### Discipline

A Discipline is a combat framework such as Guardian, Arcanist, or Duelist.

Disciplines define:

- combat mechanic/resource
- combat priorities
- manifestation access
- modifications to Weapon Arts
- specialization branches
- multiclass bridges
- hidden combat qualifications

### Divine Codex

The Active Codex is a rotating five-slot hand of extraordinary manifestations.

The Codex provides tactical spikes, reactions, spells, transformations, summons, counters, utility, and other exceptional options without replacing basic action combat.

Deck construction is the Codex's primary customization system.

The Codex should **not** also receive a giant passive skill tree.

---

## 3. Development Points and Discipline Grids

The principal conventional point-spending progression system is the **Discipline Development Grid**.

### Development Points

Character Levels and selected major accomplishments grant **Development Points**.

Prototype direction:

- most normal levels grant Development Points at a predictable cadence
- major Discipline mastery milestones may grant additional Discipline-bound insight or unlock access to special nodes
- rare discoveries may reveal nodes rather than simply grant more currency
- endgame Treasures or Authorities may eventually bend normal limits

Avoid creating many interchangeable point currencies.

### Why Development Points Exist

Development Points provide the satisfying RPG moment of:

> I leveled up. What part of my combat identity do I want to deepen?

They are not intended to replace activity-driven Skills, Jobs, equipment mastery, or hidden progression.

---

## 4. Discipline Development Grid

Each mature Discipline should have a compact Sphere-Grid-like specialization layout rather than a giant universal passive web.

Prototype mature target:

**roughly 20–35 meaningful nodes per Discipline**

The exact number is secondary to clarity and distinct playstyles.

### Node Types

#### Connector Nodes

Small improvements that make routes legible and provide modest progression.

Examples:

- slightly improved Guard efficiency
- modest resource generation improvement
- small status resistance

These should be used sparingly.

#### Behavior Nodes

Change how an action works.

Examples:

- Intercept staggers light enemies on arrival
- Perfect Guard stores impact for Shield Bash
- Sigils persist briefly after detonation
- Slipstep gains a follow-up angle bonus

These should make up much of the tree's value.

#### Build Nodes

Reward a particular play pattern.

Examples:

- ally protection
- aggressive counterplay
- battlefield control
- elemental threading
- execution windows

#### Keystone Nodes

Large identity-defining effects that require meaningful investment.

Keystones should change priorities rather than merely add large percentages.

#### Bridge Nodes

Nodes located near compatible Discipline boundaries.

When the character qualifies for both Disciplines, Bridge Nodes can connect their specialization spaces and unlock hybrid mechanics.

#### Hidden Nodes

Nodes that do not initially appear or appear only as incomplete/unknown structures.

They can be revealed through behavior, mastery, Jobs, world state, equipment, narrative decisions, or hidden qualifications.

System Comprehension controls how much information about these nodes is visible.

---

## 5. Prototype Discipline Shapes

### Guardian

Possible visible branches:

**Bulwark** — Guard, Intercept, protection, Resolve efficiency

**Vanguard** — stagger, advancing pressure, aggressive counters

**Oathkeeper** — sacrifice, ally protection, defensive commitments, hidden oath bridges

Possible hidden branch:

**Last Bastion** — revealed through extreme protection behavior, difficult survival conditions, and refusal to abandon others

### Arcanist

Possible visible branches:

**Sigilcraft** — placement, duration, geometry, chained fields

**Threading** — linking attacks and effects through established magical structures

**Battle Focus** — mobile casting, precision, defensive magical timing

Possible hidden branch:

**Law Reader** — interacts with anomalies, Dungeon Laws, unusual system metadata, or rule-modifying manifestations

### Duelist

Possible visible branches:

**Tempo** — Flow, timing, clean sequences

**Predation** — openings, execution, pursuit

**Reversal** — Deflect, angle changes, punish windows

Possible hidden branch:

**Perfect Interval** — high-precision timing and conditional combat recognition

---

## 6. Multiclass Bridge Architecture

The Development Grid should visually reinforce multiclass integration.

Example bridges:

- Guardian Vanguard ↔ Duelist Reversal
- Arcanist Battle Focus ↔ Duelist Tempo
- Guardian Oathkeeper/Bulwark ↔ Arcanist Sigilcraft

Qualifying for two Disciplines does not automatically activate every bridge.

Bridge access may require:

- investment in both relevant branches
- mastery thresholds
- specific Skills
- compatible Jobs
- equipment familiarity
- meaningful cross-system behavior
- hidden conditions

Bridge Nodes can unlock:

- Hybrid Manifestations
- modified Weapon Arts
- resource interactions
- special targeting behavior
- hidden Discipline qualifications

Multiclassing must create integrated identities rather than a larger pile of unrelated abilities.

---

## 7. Jobs

Jobs do not receive giant point trees.

Jobs are discovered identities rooted in places, cultures, institutions, environments, activities, and secrets.

Jobs progress primarily through relevant activity and can provide:

- contextual effects
- Skills
- permissions
- social/institutional recognition
- evolution routes
- hidden qualifications

Example:

Smith → Weaponsmith / Armorsmith / Field Smith

Possible hidden routes:

Smith + magical study + unusual forge access → Runeforger

Smith + monster-material practice → Monster-Smith

The world should contain opportunities to become these things rather than presenting them all in one universal menu.

---

## 8. Equipment and Mastery

Equipment is one player-facing system: **Gear**.

Individual items may gain familiarity, mastery, traits, techniques, affixes, and evolution opportunities, but the player-facing language should remain consistent:

> Use meaningful equipment in meaningful situations and deepen your relationship with it.

Equipment evolution should alter play where possible rather than only increasing statistics.

Avoid separate visible XP bars for every subcomponent unless testing proves they are useful.

---

## 9. Achievements, Titles and Treasures

### Achievements

Achievements are primarily records of accomplishment and discovery hooks.

They may occasionally unlock content, but they are not a central power progression layer.

### Titles

Titles are in-world recognitions.

They may affect:

- social reactions
- hidden qualifications
- rare events
- identity presentation

Only selected Titles should carry mechanical effects.

Titles must not become another equipment inventory of mandatory stat bonuses.

### Treasures

Treasures remain rare, limited, rule-changing artifacts.

**Equipment primarily modifies the character. Treasures may modify rules.**

Treasures should be few enough that receiving one feels important.

---

## 10. Knowledge and System Comprehension

Knowledge and System Comprehension belong to one broader player-facing domain: **Discovery**.

### Knowledge

Knowledge records what the character has learned about:

- creatures
- locations
- materials
- factions
- dungeon structures
- anomalies
- recipes
- mechanics

### System Comprehension

System Comprehension determines how clearly the character can perceive the Divine Codex and deeper system metadata.

It may reveal:

- precise status information
- hidden qualification progress
- compatibility tags
- unknown node structure
- Dungeon Law wording
- Authority signatures
- deeper classification metadata

Knowledge asks:

> What have I learned about this thing?

System Comprehension asks:

> How much of the underlying system can I perceive?

No additional general-purpose analysis progression layer should be added without a strong reason.

---

## 11. Hidden Progression

Hidden progression remains foundational but should usually run in the background.

The system tracks what the player proves through:

- behavior
- mastery
- narrative decisions
- world state
- equipment history
- failures and recovery
- combinations
- deliberate refusals or negative conditions

The Codex may eventually give that pattern a name.

Hidden progression can reveal:

- Skills
- Jobs
- Discipline branches
- Development Grid nodes
- Hybrid Manifestations
- equipment evolutions
- Titles
- Treasures
- narrative states
- eventually Authorities

Hidden unlocks should be surprising but interpretable in hindsight.

---

## 12. Advanced Manifestation Mechanics

Covenants, Compound Manifestations, Domains, and other rule-manipulation concepts are **not separate base progression systems**.

They are advanced families of Codex Manifestations.

### Covenants / Clauses

Gain power by accepting a meaningful restriction or rule.

### Compound Manifestations

Compatible manifestations combine or bind into a more complex expression.

### Domains / Local Laws

A manifestation changes a rule in a bounded area or encounter.

### Authorities

Endgame-level permissions that interact directly with deeper world laws.

Authorities should remain rare and narratively significant rather than becoming another routine progression tree.

---

## 13. Temporary Expedition Progression

Temporary expedition progression should be restrained.

Dungeon runs may contain:

- temporary tactical choices
- consumables
- temporary blessings or anomalies
- situational modifications
- unsecured loot

But the player should not rebuild an entire temporary character every dungeon.

The game's primary fantasy is persistent character development inside a living world, not repeatedly discarding the main build after each expedition.

---

## 14. Player-Facing Progression Summary

A player should be able to understand the game in these terms:

### Character

Who am I, and what can I do?

### Combat Build

How do I fight, and how have I specialized?

### Role

What has this world recognized me as being?

### Gear

What equipment have I bonded with, learned, and evolved?

### Discovery

What have I learned, and what hidden possibilities can I now perceive?

### World

How have my choices changed this timeline?

If a future feature cannot fit naturally into one of these questions, it should be treated as a potential bloat warning.

---

## 15. Vertical Slice Rule

For the Hearthcross vertical slice, implement only enough of this architecture to prove the loop.

Recommended prototype:

- Character Level with a very small number of Development Points
- one lightweight Attribute allocation moment
- one partial Discipline Grid per Guardian, Arcanist, and Duelist
- approximately 6–10 testable nodes per prototype Discipline, not the final 20–35
- one hidden node reveal
- one Job slot
- activity-driven Skills
- one equipment mastery/evolution proof
- five-slot Active Codex
- one hidden qualification
- Knowledge/System Comprehension feedback
- persistent world consequence

Do not implement full mature grids, advanced multiclass bridges, Covenants, Domains, Authorities, or large Job evolution webs before the base loop is fun.

---

## Locked Principles

1. The game uses six player-facing progression domains rather than presenting every subsystem as equal.
2. Skills improve primarily through meaningful activity and training.
3. Development Points are the main conventional point-spending currency.
4. Development Points primarily customize Discipline Development Grids.
5. Discipline Grids are compact, meaningful, and specialization-focused rather than giant passive webs.
6. Most grid nodes should alter behavior or build priorities rather than only grant percentages.
7. Hidden nodes and multiclass Bridge Nodes are part of the Discipline Grid language.
8. Jobs progress through activity, discovery, culture, institutions, and location rather than giant point trees.
9. Equipment mastery and evolution share one consistent Gear progression language.
10. Achievements and Titles are supporting systems, not major stat ladders.
11. Treasures are rare, limited, rule-changing rewards.
12. Knowledge and System Comprehension form the Discovery domain.
13. Covenants, Compound Manifestations, and Domains remain advanced Codex mechanics rather than new base systems.
14. Authorities remain rare endgame/lore architecture.
15. Temporary dungeon progression remains lighter than persistent character progression.
16. New major systems require strong justification and must survive the bloat test.
